# Timeboxing

`T6 Prioritisation and Requirements Management › 6.1 Prioritisation`

## Problem Solved

Fixes delivery deadlines and budgets, flexing scope via prioritisation rather than allowing schedules to slip, ensuring timely delivery.

## BA Lifecycle Phase

Define Requirements / Requirements Analysis / Project Execution

## Input Artifacts

Prioritised Requirements List (MoSCoW), Outer timebox schedule, product definitions.

## Output Artifacts

Timebox Plan (Kick-off/Planning, Investigate 15%, Refine 70%, Consolidate 15%, Close-out/Review), delivered product increments.

## Participants & Roles

BA, Project Manager, Development Team, Business Users / Product Owner.

## Prerequisites

Agreement that deadlines are fixed and scope is flexible using MoSCoW priorities.

## Step-by-Step Procedure

Agree fixed end date for outer/inner timebox.
2. Kick-off / Planning (select MoSCoW prioritised requirements).
3. Investigate (15% - clarify detail).
4. Refine (70% - build & test iteratively).
5. Consolidate (15% - finalize & review).
6. Close-out / Review (evaluate delivered items against goals).
7. If time runs short, drop 'Could/Should haves' to guarantee 'Must haves'.

## Analyst Questions & Prompts

What is the fixed end date for this timebox? Which 'Must haves' are included? If time runs short, which 'Should/Could haves' will we drop?

## Minimal Realistic Example

Executing a 4-week inner timebox: allocating 15% to Investigate, 70% to Refine (building stories), and 15% to Consolidate, dropping a 'Could have' report to hit the 4-week deadline.

## Quality Criteria & Verification

Timebox ends strictly on the agreed date; 100% of 'Must haves' delivered; scope flexed using lower priorities.

## Errors & Anti-Patterns

Allowing timebox deadlines to slip when work falls behind; attempting to timebox without MoSCoW prioritization; treating timeboxing as an ad-hoc panic measure.

## When NOT to Use

When fixed-scope legal compliance mandates require 100% of all listed requirements to be delivered on a single non-negotiable date.

## Alternatives & Complementary Methods

MoSCoW Prioritisation, Product Backlog, Planning Poker.

## Supported Decisions

Release scope management, iteration planning, resource allocation.

## AI & Agentic Adaptation

Project management AI engines track velocity during timeboxes, alerting teams early if 'Must have' delivery is threatened so scope flexing can occur.
