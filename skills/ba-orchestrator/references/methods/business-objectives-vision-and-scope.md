# Business Objectives, Vision and Scope

`T1 Strategy Analysis and Justification › 1.2 Strategy, Goals and Measures`

**Also described as:** Defining Business Objectives (Vision and Scope / Business Objectives Model)

## Problem Solved

Eliminates lack of alignment among stakeholders, prevents scope creep, and avoids building extraneous features that do not contribute to organizational value.

## BA Lifecycle Phase

Discovery / Analysis

## Input Artifacts

Problem Statement, executive directives, business case, project charter, market research.

## Output Artifacts

Vision and Scope Document (or Project Charter), Business Objectives Model (RML diagram), Objective Chain, Vision Statement, Success Metrics list.

## Participants & Roles

Executive Sponsor, Primary Customer, Product Visionary, Business Analyst, Product Owner/Manager, Key Decision Makers.

## Prerequisites

Understanding of the core business problem or opportunity.

## Step-by-Step Procedure

Facilitate a discussion with project sponsors and business leaders using targeted elicitation questions.
2. Extract explicit business opportunities, business objectives, and desired financial/nonfinancial outcomes.
3. Define quantitative, verifiable business objectives (stating absolute targets rather than vague relative changes).
4. Establish surrogate/interim success metrics (lagging and leading tracking indicators).
5. Draft a Product Vision Statement using the standard keyword template (For / Who / The / Is / That / Unlike / Our product).
6. (Optional) Construct a Business Objectives Model linking Business Problems to Business Objectives, Success Metrics, and Solution Concepts.
7. Document findings in a Vision and Scope document or Project Charter and obtain stakeholder alignment.

## Analyst Questions & Prompts

"What business problem are you trying to solve or what business opportunity do you perceive?", "What's the motivation for solving this problem...?", "What are your business objectives?", "How would the proposed product provide value...?", "What would a highly successful solution do for you? Can you quantify its potential payoff?", "How could you judge the solution's success?", "What could the business impact be if you don't pursue this solution?"

## Minimal Realistic Example

For a restaurant online ordering system: Problem '50% of phone requests drop off on hold' links to Objective 'Convert 95% of placement requests to submitted orders', measured by Success Metric 'Average order placement cost <= 5% of order value', mapping to Solution Concepts like 'Online menu' and 'Online order placement'.

## Quality Criteria & Verification

Objectives must be specific, realistic, absolute, and measurable (SMART). Success metrics must logically correlate with long-term business goals. Vision statement must be agreed upon by all key leaders.

## Errors & Anti-Patterns

Stating business objectives as features to build rather than business outcomes; specifying relative percentage goals without baseline references; writing vague non-verifiable targets.

## When NOT to Use

When working on minor maintenance tasks, localized bug fixes, or minor refactoring where organizational strategy is unaffected.

## Alternatives & Complementary Methods

Complementary: Practice #1 Understand Problem, Practice #3 Define Boundaries, Practice #4 Identify Stakeholders, Practice #13 Prioritization.

## Supported Decisions

Project chartering, funding/investment decisions, high-level scope inclusion/exclusion, feature prioritization alignment.

## AI & Agentic Adaptation

LLMs can ingest unstructured interview transcripts or project proposals to extract business objectives, generate candidate Vision Statements using the keyword template, draft RML models, and highlight non-measurable or vague objectives for human review.
