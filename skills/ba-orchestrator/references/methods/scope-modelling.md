# Scope Modelling

`T2 Situation Investigation and Elicitation › 2.1 Problem and Scope Framing`

## Problem Solved

Reduces scope creep and boundary ambiguity by explicitly defining what elements fall inside and outside the limits of a change, solution, or business need.

## BA Lifecycle Phase

Discovery / Planning / Strategy / architecture

## Input Artifacts

Business needs, vision statements, stakeholder lists, initial feature requests.

## Output Artifacts

Scope models (Context Diagrams, In-Scope/Out-of-Scope lists, Use Case Diagrams, Feature breakdowns, Venn diagrams).

## Participants & Roles

BA, Sponsor, Project Manager, Key Stakeholders, Systems Architect.

## Prerequisites

High-level understanding of the business need and initiative goals.

## Step-by-Step Procedure

Identify the scope objective (scope of control, need, solution, or change).
2. Select appropriate scope modelling techniques (Context Diagram, Functional Decomposition, Use Case Diagram, In/Out lists).
3. Identify boundary elements: people, processes, systems, data, and organizational units.
4. Classify elements explicitly as In-Scope or Out-of-Scope.
5. Explore relationships and dependencies between boundary elements.
6. Document critical scope assumptions and gain stakeholder agreement.

## Analyst Questions & Prompts

"Is process X explicitly included or excluded from this initiative?", "What external systems touch our solution boundary?", "What assumptions define this scope boundary?"

## Minimal Realistic Example

A Context Diagram places 'New E-Commerce Engine' in center, with In-Scope flows to 'Payment Gateway' and 'Shipping API', and explicitly marks 'ERP Accounting System' as Out-of-Scope.

## Quality Criteria & Verification

Scope boundaries are clear and agreed upon by sponsors; explicit Out-of-Scope list prevents creep; assumptions documented.

## Errors & Anti-Patterns

Failing to document Out-of-Scope items; creating high-level scope models that lack sufficient detail to guide development.

## When NOT to Use

On open-ended exploratory research initiatives where rigid boundaries impede initial ideation.

## Alternatives & Complementary Methods

Complemented by Context Diagrams, Functional Decomposition, Use Case Diagrams, and Value Stream Mapping.

## Supported Decisions

Contractual obligations, project estimation boundaries, requirement inclusion/exclusion, release planning.

## AI & Agentic Adaptation

AI agents can evaluate project communications and proposal text to draft In-Scope vs Out-of-Scope matrices, flagging boundary ambiguities for BA review.
