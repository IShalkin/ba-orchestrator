# Capability Modelling

`T1 Strategy Analysis and Justification › 1.3 Current State, Capabilities and Gaps`

**Also described as:** Business Capability Analysis; Capability modelling

This card merges 2 descriptions of the method, labelled Variant A–B in every field.

## Problem Solved

**[Variant A]**

Models what an organization needs to do to deliver value, independent of how, who, or where it is executed, reducing duplication and stability issues in business transformation.

**[Variant B]**

Reduces organizational misalignment and duplication by defining what an enterprise can do independent of how it is executed or structured.

## BA Lifecycle Phase

**[Variant A]**

Discovery / Analysis / Specification

**[Variant B]**

Discovery / analysis / prioritisation / specification

## Input Artifacts

**[Variant A]**

Corporate strategy, business architecture blueprints, functional domains overview.

**[Variant B]**

Enterprise strategy, organizational models, business goals, current state description.

## Output Artifacts

**[Variant A]**

Hierarchical Business Capability Model (Strata, Foundation, Capability Groups, Business Capabilities taxonomy).

**[Variant B]**

Capability Map, performance gap assessment, risk model, product/release roadmap.

## Participants & Roles

**[Variant A]**

BA, Enterprise Architect, Business Architect, Executive Management.

**[Variant B]**

Executive Sponsor, Business Owner, BA, Enterprise/Business Architect, SME.

## Prerequisites

**[Variant A]**

High-level understanding of the entire organizational business scope.

**[Variant B]**

Executive consensus to collaborate on a holistic enterprise model.

## Step-by-Step Procedure

**[Variant A]**

Define Strategic, Core, and Support strata (Level 0).
2. Identify Level 1 Foundation capabilities (Operational and Environmental).
3. Decompose into Level 2 Capability Groups.
4. Decompose into Level 3/4 Business Capabilities.
5. Validate uniqueness and stability using the SUAVE checklist.

**[Variant B]**

Identify and name the business capabilities required to fulfill business goals (what the business does).
2. Organize capabilities into a hierarchical Capability Map (categorized by strategic, core, supporting).
3. Assess target performance expectations against current performance to find performance gaps.
4. Evaluate business, technology, and market risks associated with capability performance.
5. Associate business value/outcomes with each capability.
6. Prioritize capability gaps to guide investment and release roadmaps.

## Analyst Questions & Prompts

**[Variant A]**

What ability does the business require to achieve this outcome? What (not how) does the business do? Is this capability unique across the entire organization?

**[Variant B]**

"What core capabilities does the organization need to deliver its value proposition?", "Where are the biggest performance gaps in our capabilities?", "Which capabilities are redundant across business units?"

## Minimal Realistic Example

**[Variant A]**

Decomposing 'HR Management' (Level 1) into 'Staff Recruitment' (Level 2) and further into 'Interviewing' and 'Induction' (Level 3).

**[Variant B]**

A retail company maps its 'Order Fulfillment' capability, identifying a 'High Performance Gap' in 'Same-Day Delivery' that justifies funding a new logistics application.

## Quality Criteria & Verification

**[Variant A]**

Capabilities are named as nouns (not verbs), contain no process flow/arrows, are unique across the enterprise, and satisfy SUAVE criteria.

**[Variant B]**

Each capability appears only once on the map; capabilities describe 'what' not 'how'; cross-functional agreement achieved on gaps.

## Errors & Anti-Patterns

**[Variant A]**

Confusing capabilities with business processes or organizational units; using verb names; introducing process sequence arrows into capability maps.

**[Variant B]**

Confusing capabilities with business processes or organizational structures; creating capability models in a silo without stakeholder buy-in.

## When NOT to Use

**[Variant A]**

When analyzing short-term procedural task sequences or temporary project task steps.

**[Variant B]**

For micro-level bug fixes or minor tactical changes where enterprise-level alignment offers no value.

## Alternatives & Complementary Methods

**[Variant A]**

Value Streams, Business Process Modelling, Value Chain Analysis.

**[Variant B]**

Complemented by Functional Decomposition, Value Stream Mapping, Business Model Canvas, and Process Architecture.

## Supported Decisions

**[Variant A]**

Target Operating Model design, enterprise architecture alignment, application portfolio rationalization, outsourcing decisions.

**[Variant B]**

Strategic investment allocation, IT portfolio prioritization, M&A integration scope, release planning.

## AI & Agentic Adaptation

**[Variant A]**

AI agents map software tool functionality and organizational processes directly to standard business capability taxonomy nodes.

**[Variant B]**

Agentic systems can process enterprise documentation to propose initial capability hierarchies and flag overlapping capability descriptions across business units for architect review.
