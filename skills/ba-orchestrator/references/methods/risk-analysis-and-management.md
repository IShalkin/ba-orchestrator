# Risk Analysis and Management

`T1 Strategy Analysis and Justification › 1.5 Business Case`

**Also described as:** Risk analysis

This card merges 2 descriptions of the method, labelled Variant A–B in every field.

## Problem Solved

**[Variant A]**

Systematically identifies, assesses (impact & probability), and formulates responses/owners for risks threatening a proposed project option, increasing business case credibility.

**[Variant B]**

Reduces the negative impact of internal and external uncertainties on project and solution value by identifying, analyzing, evaluating, and planning risk responses.

## BA Lifecycle Phase

**[Variant A]**

Evaluate Options / Prepare Business Case

**[Variant B]**

Planning / Strategy / Solution Evaluation / change management

## Input Artifacts

**[Variant A]**

Option descriptions, feasibility report, stakeholder input, risk breakdown structures.

**[Variant B]**

Business objectives, strategy, elicitation results, influences, historical project lessons learned.

## Output Artifacts

**[Variant A]**

Risk Log / Risk Matrix detailing Risk description, Impact (Large/Moderate/Small), Probability (High/Medium/Low), Response (Avoidance, Mitigation, Transference, Acceptance), and Risk Owner.

**[Variant B]**

Risk Register (Risk event, probability, impact, overall level, response strategy, risk owner, residual risk).

## Participants & Roles

**[Variant A]**

BA, Project Manager, Sponsor, Technical Lead, Risk Owner.

**[Variant B]**

BA, Project Manager, Sponsor, Risk Owner, Technical SME, Regulator.

## Prerequisites

**[Variant A]**

Selected option definition and access to key technical/business stakeholders.

**[Variant B]**

Understanding of business objectives and organizational risk tolerance levels.

## Step-by-Step Procedure

**[Variant A]**

Identify risks via interviews, workshops, or checklists.
2. Determine potential impact if risk occurs.
3. Assess scale of Impact (Large/Moderate/Small) and Probability (High/Medium/Low).
4. Formulate response actions (Avoidance, Mitigation, Transference, Acceptance).
5. Assign a specific named Risk Owner for each action.

**[Variant B]**

Risk Identification: discover potential risk events/conditions through expert judgment and elicitation.
2. Risk Analysis: estimate probability of occurrence and negative value impact (cost, timeline, quality).
3. Priority & Level: calculate overall risk level (Probability x Impact) and rank risks.
4. Risk Evaluation: compare risk levels against organizational risk tolerance thresholds.
5. Risk Treatment: select response strategy (Avoid, Transfer, Mitigate, Accept, Increase/Pursue Opportunity) and assign Risk Owner.
6. Develop response plan and continuously monitor residual risks throughout the lifecycle.

## Analyst Questions & Prompts

**[Variant A]**

What could go wrong with this option? What is the impact if it happens? How likely is it? How can we avoid or mitigate it? Who owns this risk?

**[Variant B]**

"What uncertain event could prevent us from realizing solution value?", "What is the probability and impact of risk X?", "What is our risk response strategy and who owns it?"

## Minimal Realistic Example

**[Variant A]**

Identifying risk 'Selecting wrong software package' (Large impact / Medium probability), defining Avoidance action 'Mandate rigorous requirements spec and objective selection criteria', assigned to BA Lead.

**[Variant B]**

Risk Identified: 'SME unavailable during testing' (Prob: Med, Impact: High). Mitigation Plan: 'Schedule 2-week block in advance with manager approval'; Residual Risk: Low.

## Quality Criteria & Verification

**[Variant A]**

Every identified risk has an assigned response strategy and a named individual Risk Owner with authority to act.

**[Variant B]**

Comprehensive risk coverage; actionable mitigation plans assigned to individual owners; residual risk brought within acceptable tolerance.

## Errors & Anti-Patterns

**[Variant A]**

Downplaying or hiding major risks to make the business case look artificially attractive; assigning risk ownership to people without authority.

**[Variant B]**

Logging unmanageably huge lists of trivial risks while missing critical ones; treating risk registration as a one-time exercise never updated.

## When NOT to Use

**[Variant A]**

During initial unconstrained creative brainstorming sessions.

**[Variant B]**

Never omitted entirely, though low-impact micro-tasks require only brief informal risk consideration.

## Alternatives & Complementary Methods

**[Variant A]**

Risk Management, Risk Identification, Feasibility Analysis, Force-Field Analysis.

**[Variant B]**

Complemented by Decision Analysis, SWOT Analysis, Lessons Learned, and Root Cause Analysis.

## Supported Decisions

**[Variant A]**

Business case authorization, contingency budget allocation, risk response planning.

**[Variant B]**

Change strategy selection, contingency budget allocation, solution go/no-go decisions.

## AI & Agentic Adaptation

**[Variant A]**

Risk analytics AI scans project parameters against historical project failure databases to identify candidate risks and suggest proven mitigation strategies automatically.

**[Variant B]**

AI agents can continuously monitor project health telemetry (velocity drops, turnover, code bugs) to auto-raise elevated risk flags in the Risk Register for PM review.
