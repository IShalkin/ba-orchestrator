# Organisation Modelling

`T1 Strategy Analysis and Justification › 1.3 Current State, Capabilities and Gaps`

**Also described as:** Organisation Diagram; Organizational Modelling

This card merges 2 descriptions of the method, labelled Variant A–B in every field.

## Problem Solved

**[Variant A]**

Integrates external environment factors (PESTLE, Competitors, Suppliers, Customers) with the internal Value Chain, providing complete organizational context for process improvement.

**[Variant B]**

Reduces structural misalignment and communication gaps by defining roles, responsibilities, reporting hierarchies, and formal/informal interaction networks.

## BA Lifecycle Phase

**[Variant A]**

Analyse Needs / Organisation Modelling

**[Variant B]**

Discovery / analysis / architecture / change management

## Input Artifacts

**[Variant A]**

PESTLE analysis, Value Chain, Supplier lists, Customer segments, Competitor analysis.

**[Variant B]**

Job descriptions, org charts, procedure manuals, stakeholder analysis.

## Output Artifacts

**[Variant A]**

Completed Organisation Diagram showing external forces surrounding the internal Value Chain.

**[Variant B]**

Organizational Charts (Org Charts), Matrix Models, role descriptions, interface definitions, influencer network map.

## Participants & Roles

**[Variant A]**

BA, Executive Management, Enterprise Architect, Strategy Lead.

**[Variant B]**

BA, Executive Management, HR, Business Unit Owner, SME.

## Prerequisites

**[Variant A]**

Completed PESTLE analysis and initial internal Value Chain map.

**[Variant B]**

Access to organizational structure records and key leadership stakeholders.

## Step-by-Step Procedure

**[Variant A]**

Draw central box representing the organization containing its Value Chain.
2. Map top box for PESTLE external forces.
3. Map left boxes for Resource Suppliers (People, Capital, Technology).
4. Map right boxes for Customer types (Owners, Partners, End Customers).
5. Map bottom box for Competitor organizations.
6. Draw interaction arrows.

**[Variant B]**

Identify organizational design model (Functionally-oriented, Market-oriented, or Matrix).
2. Map formal reporting relationships (who reports to whom) and organizational unit boundaries.
3. Define roles, required skills, and key responsibilities for each position.
4. Map unit interfaces (work packages and communication flows exchanged between units).
5. Identify informal lines of authority, communication, and key organizational influencers.
6. Validate model accuracy and evaluate alignment with enterprise strategy.

## Analyst Questions & Prompts

**[Variant A]**

What PESTLE factors pressure us? Who supplies our people, technology, and capital? Who competes for those suppliers and customers? How does our internal value chain deliver to those customers?

**[Variant B]**

"Who holds final operational decision authority for this department?", "Where are the silos causing cross-functional handoff delays?", "Who are the informal influencers people go to for guidance?"

## Minimal Realistic Example

**[Variant A]**

An Organization Diagram for an examination body showing external PESTLE pressures at the top, Tech/People suppliers at the left, Competitors at the bottom, and Candidates/Employers at the right, surrounding the central exam Value Chain.

**[Variant B]**

An org chart identifies that 'Customer Support' reports to 'Operations' while 'Billing' reports to 'Finance', explaining a key cross-departmental approval bottleneck.

## Quality Criteria & Verification

**[Variant A]**

Complete visual integration of external environment forces with the internal value chain activities.

**[Variant B]**

Accurately depicts both formal structures and informal influencer networks; clear boundaries and role responsibilities defined.

## Errors & Anti-Patterns

**[Variant A]**

Modeling internal organization departments inside the value chain box instead of business process activities.

**[Variant B]**

Relying strictly on outdated official org charts while ignoring informal authority structures; creating models in a vacuum without HR/management sign-off.

## When NOT to Use

**[Variant A]**

When analyzing a minor, isolated internal software utility with no external environmental dependencies.

**[Variant B]**

On small, single-team software bug fixes where organizational structures have zero impact.

## Alternatives & Complementary Methods

**[Variant A]**

Organisation Model, Value Chain Analysis, PESTLE Analysis, Context Diagram.

**[Variant B]**

Complemented by RACI Matrix, Roles and Permissions Matrix, Stakeholder Mapping, and Process Modelling.

## Supported Decisions

**[Variant A]**

Process improvement context setting, organizational alignment, project scope boundary setting.

**[Variant B]**

Organizational redesign, change management planning, governance escalation paths, role authority allocation.

## AI & Agentic Adaptation

**[Variant A]**

AI agents continually ingest external market threat data and internal process metrics to update dynamic Organization Diagrams.

**[Variant B]**

AI agents can analyze corporate communication metadata (e.g., Slack/email interaction graphs) to map informal influencer networks and cross-departmental communication bottlenecks automatically.
