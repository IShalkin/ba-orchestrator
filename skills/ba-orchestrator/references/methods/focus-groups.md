# Focus Groups

`T2 Situation Investigation and Elicitation › 2.6 Group and Creative Sessions`

**Also described as:** Focus Groups (Future Workshops)

This card merges 2 descriptions of the method, labelled Variant A–B in every field.

## Problem Solved

**[Variant A]**

Reduces customer and user adoption risk by collecting qualitative feedback, attitudes, and emotional responses about a product or topic in an interactive group setting.

**[Variant B]**

Elicits current problems, generates creative future visions, and establishes joint commitment across conflicting stakeholder groups.

## BA Lifecycle Phase

**[Variant A]**

Discovery / elicitation / Solution Evaluation

**[Variant B]**

elicitation

## Input Artifacts

**[Variant A]**

Focus group plan, discussion guide (script/questions), product prototypes/concepts, participant criteria.

**[Variant B]**

Meeting room, flipcharts/whiteboard, stakeholder list.

## Output Artifacts

**[Variant A]**

Focus group report, transcribed qualitative findings, identified user themes/attitudes.

**[Variant B]**

Prioritized list of top stakeholder issues, problems, and future wishes (with rationale 'why').

## Participants & Roles

**[Variant A]**

Moderator (unbiased), Recorder, BA, Participants (6–12 pre-qualified users/customers), Observers.

**[Variant B]**

Facilitator, log keeper, representatives from multiple stakeholder groups.

## Prerequisites

**[Variant A]**

Clearly stated session objective, pre-qualified participants matching target demographic, discussion guide.

**[Variant B]**

Selection of 6–18 diverse stakeholders.

## Step-by-Step Procedure

**[Variant A]**

Define clear objective and target participant demographics.
2. Create Focus Group Plan (logistics, location, incentives, discussion guide).
3. Assign an experienced neutral Moderator and a Scribe/Recorder.
4. Conduct 1–2 hour session: moderator leads discussion using open-ended script.
5. Recorder captures comments, non-verbal cues, and interaction dynamics.
6. Transcribe notes, analyze qualitative themes, and publish summary report.

**[Variant B]**

Invite 6–18 representatives across stakeholder groups.
2. Open session and establish ground rules (no criticism allowed).
3. Conduct roundtable on bad experiences and current problems; log on board.
4. Conduct visioning phase: Imagine the ideal future solution and capture 'why'.
5. Consolidate and edit the issue list in front of participants.
6. Have each stakeholder group select their top 10 non-prioritized issues.
7. Review selection to ensure every stakeholder group gains key benefits.

## Analyst Questions & Prompts

**[Variant A]**

"What are your immediate impressions of this product concept?", "How does feature X fit into your daily routine?", "Why do you prefer option A over option B?"

**[Variant B]**

What is the most annoying problem in your work today? If you could get whatever you wanted, what would the ideal system do and why?

## Minimal Realistic Example

**[Variant A]**

A 90-minute focus group of 8 mobile banking users tests a new app interface prototype, revealing that users find the transfer workflow confusing.

**[Variant B]**

A 3-hour session with secretaries, engineers, and IT staff producing 40 issues, resulting in secretaries prioritizing 'automatic filing by subject' as a top wish.

## Quality Criteria & Verification

**[Variant A]**

Unbiased moderation allowing all participants to speak; clear synthesis of qualitative themes; qualified demographic representation.

**[Variant B]**

Joint stakeholder agreement on top issue lists, balance of benefits across groups.

## Errors & Anti-Patterns

**[Variant A]**

Allowing one vocal participant to dominate the group; treating qualitative opinions as statistically significant quantitative data.

**[Variant B]**

Allowing technical staff to criticize user wishes during the session; taking an mathematical average of priorities across groups.

## When NOT to Use

**[Variant A]**

When quantitative statistical proof is required, or when topic matter is highly sensitive/confidential so participants won't speak openly.

**[Variant B]**

When extreme interpersonal hostility prevents stakeholders from sitting in the same room.

## Alternatives & Complementary Methods

**[Variant A]**

Complemented by Survey/Questionnaire, Interviews, Observation, and Prototyping.

**[Variant B]**

Brainstorming, Group Interviews, Tasks & Support.

## Supported Decisions

**[Variant A]**

User experience design, feature scoping, product market positioning.

**[Variant B]**

System vision freezing, high-level scope trade-offs, change management alignment.

## AI & Agentic Adaptation

**[Variant A]**

AI transcription tools can process focus group audio in real-time, performing sentiment analysis and auto-clustering user responses by theme.

**[Variant B]**

Cluster and summarize raw focus group whiteboard notes into organized problem/wish categories using LLMs.
