# Rich Pictures

`T2 Situation Investigation and Elicitation › 2.3 Boundaries and Context`

**Also described as:** Rich Pictures (Soft Systems Methodology); Rich pictures

This card merges 2 descriptions of the method, labelled Variant A–B in every field.

## Problem Solved

**[Variant A]**

Captures the holistic complexity, human relationships, emotions, culture, and structural issues of a business problem visually, reducing communication barriers created by long text.

**[Variant B]**

Clarifies ill-defined, messy problem situations involving political, social, emotional, and organizational factors before fixing boundaries.

## BA Lifecycle Phase

**[Variant A]**

Discovery / Documenting Results

**[Variant B]**

Discovery

## Input Artifacts

**[Variant A]**

Interview notes, observation logs, qualitative investigation data.

**[Variant B]**

Interviews, domain observation notes, organizational issues, stakeholder complaints

## Output Artifacts

**[Variant A]**

Rich Picture diagram capturing actors, processes, culture, conflict, and concerns.

**[Variant B]**

Rich Picture diagram (informal conceptual cartoon/drawing of domain, actors, processes, and concerns)

## Participants & Roles

**[Variant A]**

BA (Diagrammer), Stakeholders (Reviewers).

**[Variant B]**

BA, Business Owner, Sponsor, End User, SME, External Stakeholders

## Prerequisites

**[Variant A]**

Preliminary qualitative investigation of the business situation.

**[Variant B]**

Access to stakeholders and willingness to explore unstructured business contexts.

## Step-by-Step Procedure

**[Variant A]**

Identify principal actors, structures, and systems.
2. Draw pictorial representations/symbols for actors, systems, and environment.
3. Add speech bubbles, emotions, and symbols for conflicts (e.g. crossed swords) and worries (e.g. time bombs).
4. Review diagram to summarize key problem areas.

**[Variant B]**

Observe and interview people in the business domain.
2. Draw an informal picture depicting actors, processes, inputs, outputs, external bodies, and physical assets.
3. Add symbols representing human emotions, conflict points, anxieties, and unwritten rules.
4. Use the picture in stakeholder workshops to discuss perspective differences.
5. Refine the drawing until a consensus on the real problem emerges.

## Analyst Questions & Prompts

**[Variant A]**

Who are the main people involved? What worries them? Where are the conflicts or communication breakdowns? What is the organizational atmosphere?

**[Variant B]**

What is actually happening in this environment? Where are the friction points? Who feels threatened or concerned? What informal workarounds exist?

## Minimal Realistic Example

**[Variant A]**

A BA draws a Rich Picture of a sales organization showing Bob worried about money down the drain, Alice frustrated by a slow mainframe (drawn as a snail), and crossed swords between Sales and Factory.

**[Variant B]**

A restaurant rich picture showing interactions between diners, waiters, cooks, suppliers, waste management, health inspectors (concerned about rats/insects), credit card processors, and tax authorities.

## Quality Criteria & Verification

**[Variant A]**

Captures soft human and cultural aspects alongside hard process structures; easily understood by the analyst team.

**[Variant B]**

Validation through stakeholder walkthroughs; agreement that the drawing accurately captures the actual situation and core conflicts.

## Errors & Anti-Patterns

**[Variant A]**

Treating rich pictures as formal process models; expecting non-authors to understand symbols without explanation; avoiding drawing due to lack of artistic confidence.

**[Variant B]**

Making the diagram too formal or structured prematurely; omitting soft/social issues in favor of pure IT data flow.

## When NOT to Use

**[Variant A]**

When communicating formal system specifications to technical developers or presenting to overly conservative formal stakeholders who reject non-formal diagrams.

**[Variant B]**

When system boundaries, automated inputs/outputs, and operational events are already well-defined and uncontroversial.

## Alternatives & Complementary Methods

**[Variant A]**

Mind Maps, Fishbone Diagrams, Context Diagrams.

**[Variant B]**

Context Diagram, Mind Mapping, Use Case Diagram, Onion Model.

## Supported Decisions

**[Variant A]**

Problem prioritization, investigation scope, stakeholder management strategy.

**[Variant B]**

Problem definition, system boundary selection, scope of intervention.

## AI & Agentic Adaptation

**[Variant A]**

Generative image AI converts unstructured interview transcripts into rough cartoon-style rich picture sketches for analyst elaboration.

**[Variant B]**

Agentic text processing synthesizes unstructured interview logs into descriptive scene descriptions, prompting multimodal AI models to generate candidate rich pictures for review by human business analysts.
