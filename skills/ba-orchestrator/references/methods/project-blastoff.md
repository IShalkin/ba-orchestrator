# Project Blastoff

`T2 Situation Investigation and Elicitation › 2.1 Problem and Scope Framing`

## Problem Solved

Prevents projects from rushing into presumed solutions, starting on vague foundations, or solving the wrong business problem.

## BA Lifecycle Phase

Discovery

## Input Artifacts

Initial stakeholder request, business problem statement, or market opportunity.

## Output Artifacts

Goal Statement (Purpose-Advantage-Measurement), Work Context Diagram, Stakeholder Map/Matrix, Business Event List, Risk List, Business Case.

## Participants & Roles

Sponsor, Product Owner, Business Analyst, Product/Program Manager, Subject Matter Experts (SMEs), Architect, Key Stakeholders.

## Prerequisites

Access to key decision-makers and high-level business objectives/mandates.

## Step-by-Step Procedure

Define the real business problem/opportunity.
2. Establish project goals using Purpose-Advantage-Measurement (PAM).
3. Draft a Work Context Diagram to define the problem space boundary.
4. Identify and map all relevant stakeholders.
5. Discover initial business events triggering the scope.
6. Evaluate project risks, constraints, and initial cost/effort.
7. Develop the Business Case.
8. Conduct Go / No-Go decision checkpoint.

## Analyst Questions & Prompts

"What is the real business problem we are trying to solve?", "What measurable advantage does solving this bring?", "Who are the key stakeholders affected by or influencing this work?", "What external events trigger a response from this business space?", "Why is the current situation non-compliant or inefficient?"

## Minimal Realistic Example

Defining the Blastoff scope for Carmen's Taperia to reduce food order errors and delivery delays by mapping customer-waiter-kitchen interactions.

## Quality Criteria & Verification

Measurable goals established (e.g., quantifiable time/cost reduction); clean context boundary; agreed stakeholder list; positive Go/No-Go business case evaluation.

## Errors & Anti-Patterns

Presuming a specific software solution upfront; skipping Blastoff; ignoring key negative or external stakeholders; vague/unmeasurable goal statements ("make it better").

## When NOT to Use

Trivial low-risk bug fixes, routine operational maintenance, or simple predefined feature tweaks with fully known domain constraints.

## Alternatives & Complementary Methods

Agile Mini-Blastoff (iterative approach), Lean Inception, Chartering, Business Model Canvas.

## Supported Decisions

Scope boundary, project Go/No-Go, budget allocation, initial release priority, stakeholder involvement strategy.

## AI & Agentic Adaptation

Agentic prompts can automatically draft initial context diagrams, analyze historic project data for risk factors, and synthesize stakeholder interview transcripts into goal candidates; Human-in-the-Loop validates scope boundaries and makes the final Go/No-Go call.
