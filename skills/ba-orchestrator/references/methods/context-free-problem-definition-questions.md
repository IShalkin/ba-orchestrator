# Context-Free Problem Definition Questions

`T2 Situation Investigation and Elicitation › 2.1 Problem and Scope Framing`

## Problem Solved

Reduces ambiguity, misunderstandings, and misdirections when identifying the real business problem rather than jumping prematurely to a technical or biased solution.

## BA Lifecycle Phase

Discovery / Elicitation

## Input Artifacts

Initial stakeholder requests, defect reports, high-level business concerns, or initial project briefs.

## Output Artifacts

Validated problem statement, problem context, list of affected stakeholders, initial business risks, and high-level vision.

## Participants & Roles

Business Analyst (interviewer/facilitator), Problem Owner, Business Manager, SME, Executive Sponsor.

## Prerequisites

Identification of the problem owner or key business contacts authorized to discuss the business need.

## Step-by-Step Procedure

Schedule an elicitation session with the problem owner or business manager.
2. Ask non-prescriptive, context-free diagnostic questions regarding the problem, scope, impact, and non-action risks.
3. Capture the responses objectively without inserting technical or architectural assumptions.
4. Analyze the answers to separate symptoms from the root problem.
5. Draft the initial problem statement and vision.
6. Confirm the problem definition with the problem owner.

## Analyst Questions & Prompts

• What is the problem?
• What is the business justification for solving the problem?
• What are the risks associated with the issues?
• What if we don't solve the problem, or don't solve it within the deadline?
• What are the impacts to the business for any given solution?
• Are there any business constraints?
• Who is affected by the problem?
• Who owns the problem?
• When does the problem occur and how long has it been going on?
• What does it look or feel like when the problem is solved (vision)?
• How will we know that the problem is solved?
• Where in the organization does the problem exist?
• How (when) do you know it's a problem?
• What business strategy or objective is the problem/opportunity related to?
• Who is the executive decision maker or sponsor of this project?

## Minimal Realistic Example

An analyst interviews an Accounts Payable supervisor who complains about software crashing. Asking 'What if we don't solve this?' reveals that late payment penalties are accumulating. Asking 'Who is affected?' shows vendors and accounting clerks are impacted, steering the focus from a minor UI bug to vendor payment term automation.

## Quality Criteria & Verification

The resulting problem statement must be expressed in negative or constraining language, be context-free, validated by the problem owner, and distinct from any pre-selected technical solution.

## Errors & Anti-Patterns

Accepting the stakeholder's initial suggested solution as the problem statement; asking leading questions; allowing technical bias to filter responses during discovery.

## When NOT to Use

When the core problem and scope are already completely verified, baselined, and legally/operationally mandated with zero ambiguity.

## Alternatives & Complementary Methods

Complementary: The Five Whys, Problem Determination Game, Root Cause Analysis, Domain Modeling.

## Supported Decisions

Scope determination, business case approval, decision to initiate or cancel a project.

## AI & Agentic Adaptation

An AI agent can act as an automated diagnostic interviewer using these context-free prompts to intake stakeholder requests, process responses to detect implied assumptions, and synthesize a structured problem statement. Human-in-the-loop validation is required to approve the final scope.
