# Six Thinking Hats

`T3 Stakeholders › 3.3 Engagement, Conflict and Negotiation`

**Also described as:** Six Thinking Hats (Change of Perspective)

## Problem Solved

Reduces individual bias and overcome cognitive gridlock by forcing stakeholders to view a problem from six distinct standpoints.

## BA Lifecycle Phase

Elicitation / Analysis / Negotiation

## Input Artifacts

Controversial or complex requirement/problem scenario

## Output Artifacts

Multi-perspective requirement evaluations, balanced decision protocol

## Participants & Roles

Moderator, Stakeholders (adopting assigned hat perspectives)

## Prerequisites

Participants willing to temporarily adopt assigned emotional, critical, or creative perspectives.

## Step-by-Step Procedure

Introduce the problem or proposed feature.
2. Assign or collectively systematically cycle through the Six Thinking Hats (White = Facts, Red = Emotions, Black = Risks, Yellow = Benefits, Green = Creativity, Blue = Process Control).
3. Require all participants to adopt the specific hat perspective during that phase.
4. Record insights gained under each perspective.
5. Consolidate perspective findings into rounded requirements.

## Analyst Questions & Prompts

"Wearing the Black Hat, what could go wrong?", "Wearing the Yellow Hat, what are the best outcomes?"

## Minimal Realistic Example

A team stuck on deciding whether to allow dynamic route recalculation cycles through all six hats to evaluate data facts, user anxieties, failure risks, and creative options.

## Quality Criteria & Verification

Comprehensive coverage of emotional, factual, and risk aspects; consensus or empathy reached among biased stakeholders.

## Errors & Anti-Patterns

Attempting to apply it to fine-grained technical specifications, making it overly laborious.

## When NOT to Use

When detailed, fine-grained technical requirements need to be specified.

## Alternatives & Complementary Methods

Perspective-Based Reading, Role-Playing

## Supported Decisions

Scope approval, feature feasibility trade-offs, conflict resolution.

## AI & Agentic Adaptation

Multi-agent systems where individual LLM agents represent different 'hats' to simulate balanced stakeholder debates.
