# Empowered Decision Makers and Decision Rules

`T3 Stakeholders › 3.2 Stakeholder Analysis and Mapping`

**Also described as:** Identifying Empowered Decision Makers & Decision Rules

## Problem Solved

Prevents decision paralysis, recurring rework, re-litigated requirements, unmanaged priority conflicts, and sluggish project governance.

## BA Lifecycle Phase

Discovery / Governance / Change Management

## Input Artifacts

Stakeholder Profiles/Catalog, Business Objectives, Vision & Scope document, RACI matrix.

## Output Artifacts

Decision-Making Framework (Designated Decision Leaders, explicit Decision Rules per decision type, Decision Log/Record).

## Participants & Roles

Business Analyst, Product Owner (Decision Leader), Executive Sponsors, Product Champions, Key Stakeholders.

## Prerequisites

Identification and characterization of key stakeholder groups.

## Step-by-Step Procedure

Identify recurring requirements-related decision types (e.g., scope changes, conflicting priorities, quality attribute trade-offs).
2. Determine appropriate decision-makers for each category based on alignment with business objectives and constraints.
3. Appoint a designated Decision Leader (e.g., Product Owner) for each decision domain to avoid ambiguity.
4. Explicitly select and agree upon a Decision Rule for each scenario (Unanimous Vote, Consensus, Plurality Vote, Decision Leader Decides, or Delegation).
5. Establish a protocol for documenting decisions, rationale, and trade-offs.
6. Communicate decision outcomes promptly to all affected stakeholders.

## Analyst Questions & Prompts

"Who makes the final call when stakeholder needs conflict?", "How will the team reach conclusions (unanimous, consensus, leader decides)?", "What is the escalation path if a consensus cannot be reached?", "How will we justify and record decisions for stakeholders who challenge them later?"

## Minimal Realistic Example

On a multi-user-class project, user representatives from four subgroups disagree on feature workflow. The designated Product Champion for the overall user class acts as the Decision Leader under the 'Decision Leader Decides' rule, resolving the issue quickly after consulting the group.

## Quality Criteria & Verification

Decision rules and leaders are documented before major decisions arise; decisions are consistently logged with rationale; decision-makers are genuinely empowered without unannounced executive overrides.

## Errors & Anti-Patterns

Assuming team democracy without clear decision rules; endlessly seeking unanimity on minor points; delegating authority without real empowerment (overridden by hidden decision-makers); failing to document rationale.

## When NOT to Use

Always applicable; clear governance is required on every project regardless of methodology.

## Alternatives & Complementary Methods

Complementary: Practice #4 Stakeholder Identification, Practice #13 Prioritization, Practice #20 Change Management. Alternatives: Rapid/DACI framework, Delegation Poker.

## Supported Decisions

Scope trade-offs, priority ordering, release backlog acceptance, baseline change approvals.

## AI & Agentic Adaptation

AI agents can log key project decisions automatically from meeting transcripts, summarize rationale, track unresolved decision items, and analyze proposed options against stated business objectives to assist decision-makers.
