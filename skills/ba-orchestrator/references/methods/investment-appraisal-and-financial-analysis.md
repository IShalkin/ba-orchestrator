# Investment Appraisal and Financial Analysis

`T1 Strategy Analysis and Justification › 1.5 Business Case`

**Also described as:** Financial Analysis; Investment appraisal

This card merges 2 descriptions of the method, labelled Variant A–B in every field.

## Problem Solved

**[Variant A]**

Evaluates project options using quantitative financial models (Payback/Breakeven, Discounted Cash Flow / Net Present Value, Internal Rate of Return), proving financial viability.

**[Variant B]**

Reduces financial investment risk by evaluating expected costs, total cost of ownership (TCO), and projected value realization of solution options.

## BA Lifecycle Phase

**[Variant A]**

Evaluate Options / Prepare Business Case

**[Variant B]**

Strategy / prioritisation / Solution Evaluation / change management

## Input Artifacts

**[Variant A]**

Cost-Benefit Analysis figures, corporate discount rates, project timeframe constraints.

**[Variant B]**

Projected solution costs, expected benefits timeline, discount rate, operational cost estimates.

## Output Artifacts

**[Variant A]**

Financial Investment Appraisal Report showing Payback period, Net Present Value (NPV), and/or Internal Rate of Return (IRR).

**[Variant B]**

Cost-Benefit Analysis, ROI, Present Value (PV), Net Present Value (NPV), Internal Rate of Return (IRR), Payback Period.

## Participants & Roles

**[Variant A]**

BA, Finance Manager / Management Accountant, Sponsor.

**[Variant B]**

BA, Financial Analyst, Sponsor, Business Owner, Executive.

## Prerequisites

**[Variant A]**

Quantified tangible cost and benefit figures from Cost-Benefit Analysis.

**[Variant B]**

Quantified cost and benefit estimates spread across defined timeframes.

## Step-by-Step Procedure

**[Variant A]**

Compile annual cash flow table (Benefits minus Costs per year starting at Year 0).
2. Calculate Payback period (when cumulative cash flow becomes positive).
3. Apply corporate Discount Factors to calculate Discounted Cash Flows for each year.
4. Sum discounted cash flows to calculate Net Present Value (NPV).
5. Calculate Internal Rate of Return (IRR) where NPV=0 to compare against interest benchmarks.

**[Variant B]**

Estimate initial cost of change and ongoing Total Cost of Ownership (TCO).
2. Estimate annual tangible/intangible value realization across project horizon.
3. Construct Cost-Benefit Analysis table detailing net annual cash flows.
4. Apply discount rate to compute Present Value (PV) and Net Present Value (NPV = PV - Initial Investment).
5. Calculate Return on Investment (ROI = Net Benefits / Cost) and Payback Period.
6. Compare metrics against organizational hurdle rates to support solution recommendation.

## Analyst Questions & Prompts

**[Variant A]**

When does the project achieve payback? What is the Net Present Value using our corporate discount rate? What is the Internal Rate of Return compared to bank interest rates?

**[Variant B]**

"What is the Total Cost of Ownership over 5 years?", "What discount rate should be used for present value calculations?", "When will this initiative achieve breakeven (payback)?"

## Minimal Realistic Example

**[Variant A]**

Calculating an investment: Initial £410k cost with £120k net annual benefit shows 4-year Payback, but applying a 10% discount rate reveals a negative NPV of -£29,720 (failing financial appraisal).

**[Variant B]**

A $100k investment yields $40k net benefits/year for 3 years. At a 10% discount rate, PV of benefits =  $99,474, NPV = -$ 526, requiring re-evaluation.

## Quality Criteria & Verification

**[Variant A]**

Discount factors correctly aligned with corporate management accounting standards; rigorous inclusion of all tangible capital and ongoing costs.

**[Variant B]**

All financial assumptions are explicitly stated; TCO includes ongoing support/licensing; standard formulas used consistently across options.

## Errors & Anti-Patterns

**[Variant A]**

Relying solely on simple payback while ignoring the time value of money (DCF/NPV); using inaccurate discount rates; misinterpreting IRR on different scale projects.

**[Variant B]**

Ignoring ongoing maintenance/operating costs in TCO; treating intangible benefits as absolute facts without accounting for risk uncertainty.

## When NOT to Use

**[Variant A]**

For non-financial mandatory compliance projects where options are dictated by law regardless of financial return.

**[Variant B]**

For minor, non-monetary operational tweaks where financial calculation overhead outweighs the change scope.

## Alternatives & Complementary Methods

**[Variant A]**

Payback analysis, DCF/NPV, IRR, Cost-Benefit Analysis.

**[Variant B]**

Complemented by Business Cases, Decision Analysis, Estimation, and Value Stream Analysis.

## Supported Decisions

**[Variant A]**

Project authorization, capital allocation, option selection.

**[Variant B]**

Capital allocation, solution recommendation, project continuation/cancellation (stage-gate review).

## AI & Agentic Adaptation

**[Variant A]**

Financial modeling AI automatically executes multi-scenario DCF/NPV and IRR calculations, running sensitivity analyses across varying discount rates.

**[Variant B]**

AI agents can run automated NPV, IRR, and Monte Carlo sensitivity simulations across multiple cost/benefit variables, instantly producing executive financial summaries.
