# Requirements Workshops

`T2 Situation Investigation and Elicitation › 2.6 Group and Creative Sessions`

**Also described as:** Group Sessions (JAD, Brainstorming, Focus Groups); Group Sessions (JAD, Brainstorming, Workshops); Requirements Elicitation Workshops; Workshops

This card merges 7 descriptions of the method, labelled Variant A–G in every field.

## Problem Solved

**[Variant A]**

Brings key stakeholders together to discover requirements, build consensus, and make rapid decisions, reducing delay and conflicting requirements.

**[Variant B]**

Reduces project delays and conflicting stakeholder priorities by bringing key stakeholders together in a structured, facilitated event to achieve an agreed-upon goal.

**[Variant C]**

Resolves cross-departmental conflicts, aligns disparate stakeholder viewpoints, and rapidly discovers comprehensive scenarios and goals in a high-energy collaborative setting.

**[Variant D]**

Reduces single-source bias, availability bias, and cross-departmental conflicts by bringing diverse stakeholder groups together to establish shared understanding and resolve contradictory needs efficiently.

**[Variant E]**

Reduces conflicting stakeholder viewpoints, slow consensus building, and lack of cross-functional synergy regarding high-level system requirements.

**[Variant F]**

Reduces conflict between stakeholder groups, bridges organizational silos, and fosters rapid consensus and creative problem-solving.

**[Variant G]**

Fragmented stakeholder opinions

## Prerequisites

**[Variant A]**

Clear workshop objectives, selected attendees with knowledge and authority, suitable venue and tools (flipcharts, sticky notes).

**[Variant B]**

Defined workshop goal, agenda, invited key stakeholders, and room/tool logistics.

**[Variant C]**

Defined workshop purpose, selected cross-functional participants, pre-arranged room layout, and tested materials.

**[Variant D]**

Clear agenda and issue list circulated 2-3 weeks in advance; suitable room/virtual meeting setup; representation from key contrasting stakeholder groups.

**[Variant E]**

Key decision makers and cross-functional representatives are committed to attend structured workshops.

**[Variant F]**

Clear workshop scope and objectives; commitment of key stakeholders to attend full workshops.

## Step-by-Step Procedure

**[Variant A]**

Plan (define objectives, invite key attendees, arrange venue).
2. Conduct (icebreaker, focus on objectives, use discovery techniques like brainstorming/Post-it exercises).
3. Keep on track, ensure participation, and document outputs visually.
4. Summarize key decisions.
5. Follow up by issuing notes promptly and gathering feedback.

**[Variant B]**

Prepare: define goal, outcomes, agenda, select neutral facilitator/scribe, and distribute pre-read materials.
2. Opening: state purpose, desired outcomes, and establish ground rules (respect, active contribution).
3. Conduct: lead interactive exercises, facilitate collaborative discussions, and manage conflicts.
4. Maintain focus on agenda items and record deferred topics in a 'parking lot'.
5. Document decisions, outputs, and open action items in real time via scribe.
6. Wrap-up: review outcomes, assign action item owners, and distribute post-workshop documentation.

**[Variant C]**

Define workshop mission and invite a balanced cross-functional group.
2. Set up the room layout (U-shape/circle, ample wall space) and test tools.
3. Kick off with sponsor support and establish house ground rules and roles (Time-keeper, Rat-hole Watcher).
4. Execute planned collaborative activities (brainstorming, card sorting, role/action lists, acting scenes).
5. Document outputs continuously on walls/flipcharts or via digital photo capture.
6. Handle side-issues by placing them in a 'Parking Lot'.
7. Conduct a retrospective and distribute the summary report promptly for participant sign-off.

**[Variant D]**

Define a list of specific issues to resolve and set a strict time-boxed agenda including an 'other items' buffer.
2. Select participants ensuring balanced representation across stakeholder categories.
3. Circulate agenda and prep materials with sufficient lead time.
4. Set up room (U-shape/circular) or virtual tools (cameras required, quality audio).
5. Facilitate the workshop: enforce schedule, ensure quiet voices are heard, cut off long side-discussions.
6. Focus on discovering unsaid assumptions and resolving conflicting perspectives.
7. Draft an egoless, summary-focused Workshop Report highlighting decisions made and open action items.
8. Distribute report for participant review and integrate findings into the requirements repository (P.7).

**[Variant E]**

Define session objectives, roles (leader, scribe, participants), and structured templates.
2. For Brainstorming: (a) Spontaneously generate ideas without criticism/prejudice; (b) Jointly evaluate, filter, and prioritize ideas against cost/feasibility. For Structured JAD/Workshops: Execute structured agenda to draft/refine requirements collaboratively.
3. Resolve conflicting viewpoints through real-time negotiation.
4. Record decisions on wall charts/visual tools.
5. Document results in a consolidated session report.

**[Variant F]**

Define session scope, participant roles, and agenda.
2. (Brainstorming - Stage 1) Spontaneously generate diverse ideas without censorship or criticism.
3. (Brainstorming - Stage 2) Jointly evaluate, prune, and group generated ideas against agreed criteria.
4. (Structured/JAD) Walk through structured document templates or feature architectures, resolving conflicting viewpoints in real time.
5. Record consensus decisions and unresolved action items.
6. Publish workshop report for participant sign-off.

**[Variant G]**

1. Define the agenda.
2. Invite participants.
3. Run the session with exercises.
4. Capture ideas.
5. Send out the outcomes.

## Analyst Questions & Prompts

**[Variant A]**

What are the main issues we face? What ideas do you have for solving this? Do we all agree on this decision? What are our top priorities?

**[Variant B]**

"Do all participants agree on this proposed scope boundary?", "What concerns remain regarding option X?", "Who owns action item Y resulting from this decision?"

**[Variant C]**

What is the first step in this process? Does everyone agree with this definition? What could go wrong here? What is a higher priority for the business?

**[Variant D]**

"What happens next? What factors are involved? How do you currently handle this? Are there any other reasons? Did we miss anything important?"

**[Variant E]**

What creative ideas can solve this bottleneck? Do all parties agree with this trade-off? How do we rank these candidate features?

**[Variant F]**

What creative ways could solve this bottleneck? Do all departments agree with this proposed workflow? What is the relative priority of option A vs option B?

**[Variant G]**

'What common goals are we pursuing?', 'What obstacles stand in the way of the goal?'

A 1-day scenario workshop where retail staff, IT developers, and auditors use swimlanes on a wall to map an end-to-end checkout process, surfacing 12 exception scenarios and agreeing on priority fixes.

Conducting a 2-hour workshop with Claims Adjusters and Sales Managers to reconcile conflicting refund authorization thresholds, resulting in an agreed dual-signature policy.

A 2-day JAD workshop bringing together library directors, IT consultants, and student union reps to agree on electronic reservation features and fine policies.

**[Variant F]**

A 2-day JAD workshop bringing together library staff, IT consultants, and student representatives to agree on the functional scope of an automated check-out kiosk.

**[Variant C]**

## Errors & Anti-Patterns

**[Variant A]**

Inviting attendees without decision-making authority; allowing loud voices to dominate; facilitator pushing their own personal agenda; over-ambitious agenda; lacking a scribe.

**[Variant B]**

Lacking a neutral facilitator causing biased discussions; inviting too many participants (>12-15) slowing down decision making; failing to follow up on action items.

**[Variant C]**

Turning the workshop into a passive lecture/presentation; inviting too many participants (>15-20); permitting dominant personalities to hijack discussion; lack of a dedicated scribe.

**[Variant D]**

Allowing vocal personalities to dominate; producing verbatim meeting minutes instead of structured decision summaries; poor hybrid/audio setup; failing to circulate agendas in advance.

**[Variant E]**

Dominance by vocal individuals; lack of executive facilitator control; absence of key decision makers; superficial coverage of complex technical details due to time pressure.

**[Variant F]**

Dominance by vocal or senior individuals suppressing quieter experts; lack of strong facilitation leading to unstructured rambling; addressing low-level technical details prematurely.

**[Variant G]**

One participant dominates, no clear agenda

## When NOT to Use

**[Variant A]**

When stakeholders are in deep toxic conflict requiring one-on-one negotiation, or when simple factual data collection from dispersed populations is needed.

**[Variant B]**

When individual confidential feedback is needed (use Interviews) or when simple quantitative polling is sufficient (use Surveys).

**[Variant C]**

When stakeholders are extremely hostile and unwilling to communicate in a group setting, or for trivial single-user details.

**[Variant D]**

Deep-dive technical discussions involving only a single expert; highly sensitive one-on-one confidential evaluations.

**[Variant E]**

When key stakeholders cannot commit dedicated multi-day blocks, or when detailed low-level technical specification is required.

**[Variant F]**

When key stakeholders cannot commit dedicated time, or when participants have extreme interpersonal hostility requiring individual negotiation first.

**[Variant G]**

Small-scale tasks, key people unavailable
