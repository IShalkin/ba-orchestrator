# Stakeholder Identification and Analysis

`T3 Stakeholders › 3.1 Stakeholder Identification`

**Also described as:** Identifying and Characterizing Stakeholders (Stakeholder Catalog & RACI); Stakeholder Analysis; Stakeholder Analysis and Mapping; Stakeholder List, Map, or Personas

This card merges 5 descriptions of the method, labelled Variant A–E in every field.

## Problem Solved

**[Variant A]**

Identifies all individuals or groups necessary for project success, along with their goals, risks, and potential resistance.

**[Variant B]**

Reduces missing requirements, lack of user buy-in, and unaligned project expectations among varied roles.

**[Variant C]**

Reduces the risk of missing critical requirements, unstated expectations, or project failure caused by neglecting key influencers or over-focusing on vocal but low-impact individuals.

**[Variant D]**

Eliminates overlooked user classes, unvoiced regulatory/compliance constraints, missing requirements, and late-stage stakeholder friction.

**[Variant E]**

Reduces stakeholder oversight, communication breakdowns, and misalignment by identifying, categorizing, mapping, and profiling stakeholders and their characteristics.

## Step-by-Step Procedure

**[Variant A]**

Brainstorm all internal and external groups affected by the project.
2. Interview key representatives from each stakeholder role.
3. Document their primary goals, expected rewards, and perceived risks/costs.
4. Identify potential conflicts between stakeholder groups.
5. Ensure each stakeholder group receives tangible benefits in the project scope.

**[Variant B]**

Identify key stakeholder categories (customers, users, managers, technical team).
2. Record organizational role and relationship to project.
3. Determine specific needs, concerns, and perspectives of each category.
4. Identify representative individuals to participate in elicitation and review.
5. Plan involvement and feedback channels.

**[Variant C]**

Identify all potential stakeholder categories on both target and production sides.
2. Evaluate each group's influence (ability to affect or be affected by the project/system) and availability.
3. Plot stakeholders onto the 2x2 grid (Key Stakeholders, Shadow Influencers, Fellow Travelers, Observers).
4. Formulate engagement strategies: prioritize Key Stakeholders and actively seek out Shadow Influencers.
5. Document category definitions and key representatives in Chapter G.7 of the Goals Book.
6. Re-evaluate stakeholder map periodically during requirements evolution.

**[Variant D]**

Brainstorm candidate stakeholders using standard categories (development team, inside organization, outside organization).
2. Review org charts, Vision Statement 'For' clauses, and historical stakeholder catalogs.
3. Segment direct and indirect users into distinct User Classes based on task frequency, privilege level, or experience.
4. Identify Favored User Classes whose needs align most closely with business objectives.
5. Characterize each stakeholder group using a profile template (Roles, Interests, Influence, Needs, Constraints).
6. Designate specific empowered individual representatives (e.g., Product Champions).
7. (Optional) Create a RACI Matrix specifying who is Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, and Informed for requirements activities.

**[Variant E]**

Brainstorm and list all internal/external stakeholders affected by or influencing the change.
2. Analyze characteristics: authority, attitude/interest, influence, decision authority.
3. Map stakeholders using matrices (Influence vs Impact) or Onion Diagrams (level of involvement).
4. Assign responsibility levels using a RACI matrix (Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed).
5. Develop Personas (archetypal fictional user profiles) for major end-user groups.
6. Formulate tailored collaboration, communication, and engagement strategies.

## Analyst Questions & Prompts

**[Variant A]**

Who pays for the system? Who will use it daily? What do you gain or fear from this new system?

**[Variant B]**

Who is impacted by this system? Who will use it directly? Who pays for it? Who maintains or tests it?

**[Variant C]**

"Who will use the system? Who will be affected by its deployment? Who makes acceptance decisions? Whose non-involvement could derail the project?"

**[Variant D]**

"Who has influence or control over the project's budget and schedule?", "Who will be using the product directly? Indirectly?", "Who is responsible for other systems or projects that yours will affect?", "Who could have legal, compliance, regulatory, or process influence?", "Whose business processes would the system affect?", "Who is expected to supply data?"

**[Variant E]**

"Who is impacted directly/indirectly by this change?", "What is stakeholder X's level of influence and interest?", "What are the key goals and pain points of Persona Y?"

## Minimal Realistic Example

**[Variant A]**

Matrix identifying Sponsor (wants cost savings), Receptionists (fear complex UI, want easy check-in), and IT Dept (want low maintenance).

**[Variant B]**

Identifying Loan Officers (need quick evaluation tools), Applicants (need simple submission forms), and IT Maintenance (need automated backups).

**[Variant C]**

Mapping a university course management system: Instructors & Students = Key Stakeholders; Sysadmins = Shadow Influencers (high impact, busy); Enthusiastic student rep = Fellow Traveler; Legal = Observer.

**[Variant D]**

For the Speak-Out.biz platform, stakeholders are categorized into Author (writes articles, high interest, low power), Reader, Publication Editor, and Administrator. Authors are designated as a primary user class needing an easy text editor and statistics.

**[Variant E]**

An Influence/Impact matrix maps 'Finance Director' as High Influence/High Impact (Key Player - closely engage), and 'Field Reps' as Low Influence/High Impact (Keep informed/consult).

## Quality Criteria & Verification

**[Variant A]**

Sponsor review, stakeholder coverage check.

**[Variant B]**

Review stakeholder list to ensure direct/indirect roles, active champions, and end-user representatives are all accounted for.

**[Variant C]**

Coverage completeness check: ensuring no critical category (e.g. testers, legal, client programmers) is omitted; verification against Chapter G.7.

**[Variant D]**

Ensure every user class has designated, available human representatives or proxies; verify that favored user classes are explicitly identified; check for complete coverage across legal, operational, and technical roles.

**[Variant E]**

Exhaustive coverage of affected parties; RACI assigns exactly 1 Accountable role per task; Personas grounded in real user research data.

## Errors & Anti-Patterns

**[Variant A]**

Only consulting appointed middle managers while ignoring daily end users who operate the system.

**[Variant B]**

Ignoring operational/maintenance stakeholders or letting developers dictate user requirements without direct end-user input.

**[Variant C]**

Confounding individual persons with stakeholder categories; neglecting Shadow Influencers (e.g. sysadmins); over-catering to 'vocal' Fellow Travelers; treating the map as static.

**[Variant D]**

Treating users as a monolithic group ('the user'); relying on non-empowered or unavailable surrogates; failing to involve compliance/legal early; overlooking indirect users or process workers.

**[Variant E]**

Forgetting key stakeholder groups leading to late requirements; treating stakeholder analysis as a static one-time activity.

## When NOT to Use

**[Variant A]**

None; applicable to all projects.

**[Variant B]**

Never omitted; essential for all non-trivial systems.

**[Variant C]**

Small single-user applications with trivial stakeholder landscapes.

**[Variant D]**

Always applicable; every software project has stakeholders.

**[Variant E]**

Rarely omitted, though minimal lightweight listing is sufficient for simple single-user tasks.
