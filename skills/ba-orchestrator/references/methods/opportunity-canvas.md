# Opportunity Canvas

`T1 Strategy Analysis and Justification › 1.2 Strategy, Goals and Measures`

**Also described as:** Opportunity Canvas / Assessment

## Problem Solved

Reduces strategic uncertainty and risk of building features that fail to achieve business impact or solve real user problems; prevents accepting low-value ideas into product development.

## BA Lifecycle Phase

Discovery / prioritisation

## Input Artifacts

Product ideas, stakeholder suggestions, market observations, business metrics.

## Output Artifacts

Opportunity Canvas (9-box structure summarizing problems, users, solutions, user value, metrics, adoption, business metrics, and budget), Go/No-Go decision.

## Participants & Roles

Product Owner / Product Manager, Core Discovery Team (UX Designer, Senior Engineer), Business Stakeholders, SMEs.

## Prerequisites

A proposed feature, product, or enhancement idea requiring evaluation.

## Step-by-Step Procedure

Identify the proposed Solution Ideas or underlying Problems.
2. Define target Users & Customers.
3. Detail Solutions Today (competitors/workarounds).
4. Outline User Value and User Metrics.
5. Define Adoption Strategy.
6. Identify Business Problems and Business Metrics.
7. Determine Development Budget.
8. Make a Go/No-Go (Trash/Proceed to Deep Discovery) decision.

## Analyst Questions & Prompts

"What problems do prospective users have today?", "How do users address their problems today?", "If users have this solution, how will they do things differently?", "What business performance metrics will be affected?", "How much money/time would we budget to discover and build this?"

## Minimal Realistic Example

Evaluating a new direct email promotion feature for band managers: defining the target user (Band Manager), the problem (promoting gigs efficiently to fans), current workaround (manual emails), success metrics (open rates and ticket sales), and setting a fixed discovery/development budget.

## Quality Criteria & Verification

Alignment across the 9 boxes; agreement on target metrics; consensus on Go/No-Go decision by product leadership; validation of user problems against real feedback.

## Errors & Anti-Patterns

Filling out the canvas as a bureaucratic form rather than a discussion framework; treating assumptions as facts without testing; failing to make tough 'No-Go' (trash) decisions.

## When NOT to Use

Small bug fixes, clear maintenance tasks, well-understood minor refinements with minimal cost and risk.

## Alternatives & Complementary Methods

Marty Cagan's Opportunity Assessment, Lean Canvas, Business Model Canvas, Journey Mapping.

## Supported Decisions

Go/No-Go discovery initiation, budget allocation, target customer segment selection, metric alignment.
