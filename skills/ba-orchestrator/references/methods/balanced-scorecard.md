# Balanced Scorecard

`T1 Strategy Analysis and Justification › 1.2 Strategy, Goals and Measures`

**Also described as:** Balanced Business Scorecard

This card merges 2 descriptions of the method, labelled Variant A–B in every field.

## Problem Solved

**[Variant A]**

Balances financial metrics with non-financial drivers of future success, reducing short-sighted over-reliance on past financial performance.

**[Variant B]**

Mitigates narrow financial focus by balancing performance evaluation across four perspective dimensions: Financial, Customer, Business Process, and Learning & Growth.

## BA Lifecycle Phase

**[Variant A]**

Analysis / Performance Measurement / Strategy Implementation

**[Variant B]**

Discovery / analysis / prioritisation / change management

## Input Artifacts

**[Variant A]**

Corporate Strategy, CSFs and KPIs, Mission and Objectives.

**[Variant B]**

Enterprise vision, strategic plans, business goals and objectives, operational performance metrics.

## Output Artifacts

**[Variant A]**

Balanced Business Scorecard (BBS) covering Financial, Customer, Internal Business Process, and Learning & Growth perspectives.

**[Variant B]**

Balanced Scorecard matrix/dashboard, strategy map, target KPIs, performance reports.

## Participants & Roles

**[Variant A]**

BA, Executive Management, Sponsor, Finance Director, Operational Leads.

**[Variant B]**

Executive Sponsor, Business Owner, BA, Product Manager, Operational Support.

## Prerequisites

**[Variant A]**

Defined corporate vision, strategy, and strategic objectives.

**[Variant B]**

A clearly articulated organizational vision and strategic plan.

## Step-by-Step Procedure

**[Variant A]**

Review corporate Vision and Strategy.
2. Define CSFs/KPIs for the Financial perspective.
3. Define CSFs/KPIs for the Customer perspective.
4. Define CSFs/KPIs for the Internal Business Process perspective.
5. Define CSFs/KPIs for Learning and Growth (Innovation).
6. Monitor performance holistically across all four quadrants.

**[Variant B]**

Translate vision and strategy into tangible strategic objectives.
2. Categorize objectives across 4 dimensions: Financial, Customer, Internal Business Process, Learning & Growth.
3. Define leading and lagging measures (KPIs) for each objective.
4. Establish baseline values and target metrics for specified timeframes.
5. Define strategic initiatives to achieve the target outcomes.
6. Continuously monitor progress and adapt strategy as needed.

## Analyst Questions & Prompts

**[Variant A]**

How do we look to shareholders (Financial)? How do customers see us (Customer)? What must we excel at (Internal Business Process)? How can we continue to improve and create value (Learning & Growth)?

**[Variant B]**

"To succeed financially, how should we appear to shareholders?", "To achieve our vision, how should we appear to our customers?", "What business processes must we excel at?", "How will we sustain our ability to change and improve?"

## Minimal Realistic Example

**[Variant A]**

A company measures supplier costs (Financial), price competitiveness (Customer), quality checking process speed (Internal Process), and new product introduction rates (Learning & Growth).

**[Variant B]**

An e-commerce firm sets target KPIs: Financial (+15% ROI), Customer (90% satisfaction score), Process (order fulfillment < 24 hrs), Learning (100% staff trained on new platform).

## Quality Criteria & Verification

**[Variant A]**

Balanced inclusion of quantitative metrics across all four perspectives, avoiding dominance by financial metrics.

**[Variant B]**

Measures are quantitative, explicitly linked to enterprise strategy, balanced between leading/lagging indicators, and clearly communicated.

## Errors & Anti-Patterns

**[Variant A]**

Focusing exclusively on financial outcomes; treating the scorecard as a static reporting dashboard rather than an active strategic management tool.

**[Variant B]**

Treating the scorecard as a static reporting tool rather than an active strategic management framework; lacking a clear strategy to align the four dimensions.

## When NOT to Use

**[Variant A]**

For single minor feature enhancement projects without broad organizational reach.

**[Variant B]**

For small, isolated tactical projects where organizational strategic alignment is not relevant or where no overarching business strategy exists.

## Alternatives & Complementary Methods

**[Variant A]**

CSFs and KPIs, MOST Analysis.

**[Variant B]**

Complemented by SWOT Analysis, Benchmarking, Metrics & KPIs, and Business Model Canvas.

## Supported Decisions

**[Variant A]**

Strategic performance monitoring, project portfolio selection, business model evaluation.

**[Variant B]**

Strategic investment decisions, project portfolio prioritization, organizational alignment, operational change strategy.

## AI & Agentic Adaptation

**[Variant A]**

AI analytics engines compile cross-departmental data into unified Scorecard views, predicting future performance shifts across all four quadrants.

**[Variant B]**

Agentic systems can ingest operational data logs to dynamically calculate leading indicators, detect cross-dimensional performance variances, and simulate strategy adjustments with human executive sign-off.
