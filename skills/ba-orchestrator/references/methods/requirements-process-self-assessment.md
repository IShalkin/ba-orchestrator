# Requirements Process Self-Assessment

`T6 Prioritisation and Requirements Management › 6.6 Requirements Risk and Process Improvement`

**Also described as:** Requirements Process Self-Assessment and Action Planning

## Problem Solved

Prevents recurring project failures, schedule overruns, and quality defects by systematically diagnosing organizational BA process weaknesses and executing goal-oriented process improvements.

## BA Lifecycle Phase

change management / BA process improvement

## Input Artifacts

Current Practice Questionnaire / Assessment Tool, project retrospective findings, defect logs.

## Output Artifacts

Assessment Results, Cause-and-Effect (Fishbone) Diagrams, Process Improvement Action Plan, Process Improvement Road Map.

## Participants & Roles

Lead Business Analyst / Process Improvement Owner, BA Team Members, Development/Testing/Business Representatives, Executive Sponsor.

## Prerequisites

Management commitment to allocate time and resources for process improvement.

## Step-by-Step Procedure

Conduct self-assessment using structured questionnaires/interviews across multiple BAs and stakeholders.
2. Identify top pain points and analyze root causes using Cause-and-Effect (Fishbone) diagrams.
3. Select high-leverage improvement goals aligned with business value (e.g., reduce requirements rework).
4. Draft a focused Process Improvement Action Plan (3–10 action items) with assigned owners, deliverables, and metrics.
5. Create or adapt requirements process assets (templates, checklists, procedures).
6. Pilot new processes on a small, non-critical project and collect participant feedback.
7. Refine assets based on pilot results and roll out to the wider organization.
8. Evaluate Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) to confirm pain points are reduced.

## Analyst Questions & Prompts

What were the root causes of rework on previous projects? Which current practices score lowest on the self-assessment? What specific business goals will this process change support? What metrics indicate success?

## Minimal Realistic Example

Executing a 20-question self-assessment, identifying missing change control as a key root cause of schedule slips, drafting an action plan to pilot a CCB charter and template on Project A, and reducing requirements volatility.

## Quality Criteria & Verification

Action items have single named owners and target dates; pilots validate practical utility before rollout; KPIs show quantitative improvement post-implementation.

## Errors & Anti-Patterns

Attempting too many process changes simultaneously; treating process improvement as a one-time document creation exercise without piloting; abandoning improvements during the initial productivity drop ('valley of despair').

## When NOT to Use

When organization leadership refuses to provide time or resources to modify existing workflows.

## Alternatives & Complementary Methods

Project Retrospectives, CMMI Maturity Assessments, Goal-Question-Metric (GQM) Framework.

## Supported Decisions

Organizational BA standard adoption, tool procurement decisions, training and resource allocation.
