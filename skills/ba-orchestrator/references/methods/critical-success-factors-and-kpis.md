# Critical Success Factors and KPIs

`T1 Strategy Analysis and Justification › 1.2 Strategy, Goals and Measures`

**Also described as:** Critical success factors and key performance indicators; Metrics and Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

This card merges 2 descriptions of the method, labelled Variant A–B in every field.

## Problem Solved

**[Variant A]**

Defines vital areas of performance and specific quantitative metrics to monitor progress toward organizational objectives, reducing ambiguity in performance evaluation.

**[Variant B]**

Reduces performance evaluation subjectivity by defining quantifiable indicators to track solution performance and strategic progress over time.

## BA Lifecycle Phase

**[Variant A]**

Discovery / Analysis / Performance Measurement

**[Variant B]**

Strategy / Solution Evaluation / analysis / change management

## Input Artifacts

**[Variant A]**

MOST Analysis, CATWOE, Balanced Business Scorecard, corporate objectives.

**[Variant B]**

Business objectives, strategic goals, current baseline metrics, solution specs.

## Output Artifacts

**[Variant A]**

CSF list (Industry-wide and Organization-specific), KPI targets with defined timeframes.

**[Variant B]**

KPI framework, target metrics, monitoring & evaluation plan, performance dashboards/reports.

## Participants & Roles

**[Variant A]**

BA, Sponsor, Executive Leadership, Performance Manager.

**[Variant B]**

BA, Business Owner, Executive Sponsor, Operational Support, Data Analyst.

## Prerequisites

**[Variant A]**

Clear definition of organizational Mission and Objectives.

**[Variant B]**

Clear business goals, established baseline metrics, and data collection mechanisms.

## Step-by-Step Procedure

**[Variant A]**

Review corporate MOST and executive core beliefs (CATWOE).
2. Identify industry-wide Critical Success Factors (CSFs).
3. Identify organization-specific differentiator CSFs.
4. Define specific Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) for each CSF.
5. Set quantitative targets and timeframes for each KPI.

**[Variant B]**

Identify business concerns, goals, and strategic objectives.
2. Define good indicators (clear, relevant, economical, adequate, quantifiable, trustworthy).
3. Establish target metrics (points, thresholds, or ranges) and timeframes.
4. Design data collection, analysis, and reporting procedures.
5. Gather baseline data prior to change implementation.
6. Continuously track actual performance metrics against baselines and targets.

## Analyst Questions & Prompts

**[Variant A]**

What critical factors are essential for our business to succeed? What specific metric measures if we achieved this success factor? What is the exact target and timeframe?

**[Variant B]**

"What specific indicator proves that objective X has been achieved?", "What is our baseline starting point for metric Y?", "Is this indicator reliable and economical to collect?"

## Minimal Realistic Example

**[Variant A]**

For the CSF 'Excellent Customer Service', defining KPIs: 'Fewer than 2% customer complaints on quarterly survey' and 'More than 60% repeat purchases within 12 months'.

**[Variant B]**

Target KPI for a helpdesk portal: 'Reduce average ticket resolution time from baseline 48 hours to target < 12 hours within 6 months.'

## Quality Criteria & Verification

**[Variant A]**

KPIs must be specific, measurable, actionable, relevant, and time-bound (SMART), directly linked to identified CSFs.

**[Variant B]**

Indicators are valid, reliable, and timely; targets align directly with higher-level strategic goals.

## Errors & Anti-Patterns

**[Variant A]**

Creating KPIs that do not tie back to strategic CSFs; setting unmeasurable or vague performance targets; tracking too many vanity metrics.

**[Variant B]**

Collecting excessive 'vanity' metrics that add collection overhead without driving decisions; perverse incentives where users game the metric.

## When NOT to Use

**[Variant A]**

When managing ad-hoc, exploratory research projects where quantifiable outcomes cannot be pre-defined.

**[Variant B]**

When data collection costs vastly outweigh the value of monitoring, or when measuring purely ad-hoc creative activities.

## Alternatives & Complementary Methods

**[Variant A]**

Balanced Business Scorecard, Outcome Frame.

**[Variant B]**

Complemented by Balanced Scorecard, Value Stream Mapping, Solution Performance Analysis, and Benchmarking.

## Supported Decisions

**[Variant A]**

Performance management, business case evaluation, project success verification.

**[Variant B]**

Solution performance evaluation, strategy adjustment, process optimization investments.

## AI & Agentic Adaptation

**[Variant A]**

Autonomous monitoring agents stream real-time operational data to generate predictive KPI alerts and flag potential CSF breaches before they occur.

**[Variant B]**

Agentic systems can continuously monitor live telemetry data, auto-detecting KPI trend anomalies and triggering real-time alerts or corrective process flows.
