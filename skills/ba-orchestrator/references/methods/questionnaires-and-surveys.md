# Questionnaires and Surveys

`T2 Situation Investigation and Elicitation › 2.5 Interviews, Questionnaires and Individual Techniques`

**Also described as:** Questionnaire; Questionnaires; Survey or Questionnaire; Surveys

This card merges 5 descriptions of the method, labelled Variant A–E in every field.

## Problem Solved

**[Variant A]**

Reduces uncertainty regarding stakeholder perceptions, subjective preferences, and priority distributions across a large or geographically distributed user base.

**[Variant B]**

Reduces subjective uncertainty and collects feedback or preferences quickly from a large, geographically distributed stakeholder population at low cost.

**[Variant C]**

Gathers a large magnitude of information from many stakeholders quickly at low cost.

**[Variant D]**

Reduces data collection costs and time bottlenecks when gathering structured feedback, customer preferences, or work practice data from large or geographically dispersed groups.

**[Variant E]**

Gathers quantitative and qualitative data from a large, geographically dispersed population, reducing sampling bias and validating findings from small sample groups.

## Prerequisites

**[Variant A]**

Representative target sample identified; well-defined topics requiring quantitative or weighted subjective input.

**[Variant B]**

Clear understanding of the objective of the survey; identified target respondent sample.

**[Variant C]**

Clear domain understanding; knowledge of psychological guidelines for creating questionnaires.

**[Variant D]**

Clear objective, identified target population, and distribution channel.

**[Variant E]**

Clear survey objectives, defined population sample, distribution mechanism (e.g., web tool).

## Step-by-Step Procedure

**[Variant A]**

Identify the target group and required information.
2. Design closed-ended, weight-based, or multiple-choice questions.
3. Include redundant/control questions to detect inconsistencies.
4. Pre-validate and pilot the questionnaire for clarity and non-ambiguity.
5. Distribute to target sample.
6. Collect responses, filter out inconsistent answers, and analyze statistical findings.

**[Variant B]**

Determine the specific information or preference to be elicited.
2. Design questions (multiple-choice, weighting scales, or short answers) avoiding bias and ambiguity.
3. Pre-validate the questionnaire with a small sample group.
4. Distribute the questionnaire to the representative sample.
5. Collect, aggregate, and analyze responses to inform interviews or requirements prioritization.

**[Variant C]**

Define known requirements or conjectures to be assessed.
2. Formulate open and/or closed (multiple-choice) questions.
3. Distribute questionnaire (e.g., via an online platform) to target stakeholders.
4. Stakeholders fill out and submit responses.
5. Aggregate and evaluate submitted data.

**[Variant D]**

Define clear survey objective and target survey population.
2. Select question types: Close-ended (quantitative, easy to analyze) and Open-ended (qualitative insights).
3. Determine sample group size and distribution method (online, email, phone).
4. Write clear, non-leading questions and conduct usability test/pilot on survey.
5. Distribute survey with clear deadline, purpose statement, and confidentiality assurance.
6. Collate responses, perform statistical analysis on quantitative items, categorize qualitative themes, and publish summary report.

**[Variant E]**

Define purpose and scope.
2. Design structure (Title, Heading/Incentive, Classification, Data section).
3. Draft unambiguous open, closed, and limited-choice questions.
4. Pilot test questionnaire on a small sample.
5. Distribute survey.
6. Analyze collected responses using mean/mode/statistical tools.

## Analyst Questions & Prompts

**[Variant A]**

Which features do you rank as highest priority? On a scale of 1 to 5, how critical is feature X? How frequently do you encounter issue Y?

**[Variant B]**

Which feature is most critical to your daily work? On a scale of 1-5, how severe is problem X? Which option do you prefer: A or B?

**[Variant C]**

Open and closed/multiple-choice questions inquiring about stakeholder assessments and requirements.

**[Variant D]**

"On a scale of 1-5, how satisfied are you with feature X?", "Which of the following options best describes your daily usage?", "What specific improvements would you suggest?"

**[Variant E]**

How satisfied are you with the current process (Scale 1-5)? Which feature do you use most frequently? What is your main daily operational issue?

## Minimal Realistic Example

**[Variant A]**

Distributing a online survey to 500 university students to rate their preference for borrowing physical books versus accessing digital libraries.

**[Variant B]**

Sending a 5-question weighting survey to 200 university faculty members to rank the importance of online journal access vs. physical book reservations.

**[Variant C]**

An online survey sent to 200 drivers asking them to rate the importance of dynamic traffic avoidance versus manual route selection on a scale of 1-5.

**[Variant D]**

An online survey with 10 closed-ended questions sent to 500 remote store managers yields a 60% response rate, proving that 80% want automated inventory replenishment.

**[Variant E]**

A BA distributes an online survey to 500 remote sales reps to validate whether workshop claims regarding CRM slowness are shared across all regions.

## Quality Criteria & Verification

**[Variant A]**

Pre-validation for non-ambiguity; representativeness and statistical significance of target respondents; coverage of options; absence of bias.

**[Variant B]**

Statistical significance of responses, absence of leading/biased questions, non-ambiguity validation prior to distribution.

**[Variant C]**

Statistical validity, non-ambiguous questions, high response rate.

**[Variant D]**

Questions are unbiased and unambiguous; statistical sample size is representative of target population; high response rate achieved.

**[Variant E]**

High response rate, representative population sample, pre-tested unambiguous questions.

## Errors & Anti-Patterns

**[Variant A]**

Biased or opinionated formulations; overly open-ended prompts causing low response rate; sample selection bias; misinterpretation of prompts by respondents.

**[Variant B]**

Biased sampling or low response rates leading to distorted conclusions; ambiguous wording leading to conflicting interpretations; relying solely on questionnaires without follow-up interviews.

**[Variant C]**

Poorly formulated or forgotten questions; inability to receive immediate feedback or discover completely unknown requirements.

**[Variant D]**

Writing ambiguous or leading questions that bias results; surveying an unrepresentative sample group.

**[Variant E]**

Asking overly complex or ambiguous questions; forcing binary answers without a 'Don't know' or comments option; low response rates leading to non-responder bias.

## When NOT to Use

**[Variant A]**

When deep qualitative exploration, complex process discovery, or domain learning is required.

**[Variant B]**

When deep, qualitative exploration of complex, unstructured, or tacit knowledge is required.

**[Variant C]**

When trying to discover completely novel/unconscious delighter requirements or when immediate interactive feedback is required.

**[Variant D]**

When deep, open-ended qualitative exploration or complex back-and-forth negotiation is required (use Interviews or Workshops instead).

**[Variant E]**

As a primary tool for discovering complex, ambiguous business rules or resolving deep stakeholder political conflicts.

## Alternatives & Complementary Methods

**[Variant A]**

Interviews, group sessions/workshops, card sorting.

**[Variant B]**

Complementary: Follow-up Structured Interviews. Alternative: Focus Groups, Brainstorming.

**[Variant C]**

Interview, Workshops

**[Variant D]**

Alternatives/Complements include Focus Groups, Interviews, Observation, and Metrics & KPIs.

**[Variant E]**

Interviewing, Workshops, Sampling, Special-Purpose Records.

**[Variant B]**

Feature prioritization, user preference selection, scope trade-offs.

**[Variant C]**

Prioritisation and validation of existing requirement hypotheses across large user populations.

**[Variant D]**

Customer satisfaction evaluation, feature prioritization, broad operational gap identification.

**[Variant E]**

Requirements baseline validation, user prioritization, pain-point quantification.

## AI & Agentic Adaptation

**[Variant A]**

AI agents can generate unbiased survey questions based on preliminary topic goals, auto-analyze free-text survey responses, and flag statistical anomalies.

**[Variant B]**

AI agents can automatically generate unbiased survey questions based on identified gaps, analyze open-ended text responses using NLP, and summarize sentiment.

**[Variant C]**

LLM agents can automatically compile balanced questionnaires from existing domain documentation and analyze statistical distributions of responses.

**[Variant D]**

AI agents can design survey questions based on research goals, auto-analyze free-text survey responses using NLP sentiment analysis, and generate statistical chart summaries.

**[Variant E]**

AI tools generate optimized survey questions based on project goals and automatically perform sentiment and statistical analysis on open-text responses.
