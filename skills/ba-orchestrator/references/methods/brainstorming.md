# Brainstorming

`T2 Situation Investigation and Elicitation › 2.6 Group and Creative Sessions`

This card merges 2 descriptions of the method, labelled Variant A–B in every field.

## Problem Solved

**[Variant A]**

Mitigates creative blocks and limited perspectives by producing a broad, non-judgmental set of ideas and options for problem solving or feature generation.

**[Variant B]**

Collects a large volume of creative ideas quickly and fosters collaborative idea expansion across diverse stakeholder groups.

## BA Lifecycle Phase

**[Variant A]**

Discovery / elicitation / analysis

**[Variant B]**

Elicitation / Discovery

## Input Artifacts

**[Variant A]**

Problem statement, area of interest definition, evaluation criteria.

**[Variant B]**

Initial problem statement or vision

## Output Artifacts

**[Variant A]**

Categorized and rated idea list, action item list, mind map/affinity diagram.

**[Variant B]**

Categorized and analyzed list of ideas

## Participants & Roles

**[Variant A]**

Facilitator, Scribe, BA, Sponsor, SME, End User, Developer, Tester (6–8 participants recommended).

**[Variant B]**

Moderator, Stakeholders (group of 5 to 10 people)

## Prerequisites

**[Variant A]**

A clearly defined topic or problem statement and pre-agreed evaluation criteria.

**[Variant B]**

Balanced group dynamics; willingness to withhold early criticism.

## Step-by-Step Procedure

**[Variant A]**

Develop a concise definition of the area of interest and establish time limits.
2. Select participants representing diverse backgrounds and set ground rules (no criticism during generation).
3. Conduct session: share and visibly record ideas rapidly without discussion or judgment.
4. Encourage participants to build on and combine each other's ideas.
5. Wrap up: review, discuss, and evaluate ideas against criteria.
6. Consolidate, eliminate duplicates, rate ideas, and distribute final list.

**[Variant B]**

Assemble a group of 5 to 10 stakeholders.
2. State the objective or problem to be addressed.
3. Collect ideas within a specified time frame.
4. Moderator documents ideas without discussing, judging, or commenting.
5. Participants build upon and modify each other's ideas.
6. Analyze and structure the collected ideas post-session.

## Analyst Questions & Prompts

**[Variant A]**

"What options are available to resolve this issue?", "What factors are constraining us?", "What crazy or exaggerated ideas might spark a practical solution?"

**[Variant B]**

"What features or capabilities could solve this problem?", "How can we expand on this suggested idea?"

## Minimal Realistic Example

**[Variant A]**

A 30-minute cross-functional workshop generates 45 ideas to reduce customer checkout drop-offs, which are then grouped and rated to select the top 3 for prototyping.

**[Variant B]**

A 10-person cross-functional team meets to generate ideas on how a navigation system should react when GPS connectivity is completely lost.

## Quality Criteria & Verification

**[Variant A]**

High volume of diverse ideas generated; non-judgmental atmosphere maintained during generation; clean rated output produced.

**[Variant B]**

Quantity of ideas generated, level of multi-stakeholder participation, unbiased initial collection.

## Errors & Anti-Patterns

**[Variant A]**

Debating or criticizing ideas during the generation phase; dominance by vocal participants suppressing reserved team members.

**[Variant B]**

Premature evaluation/judgment of ideas; muddled group dynamics dominated by loud or high-authority individuals.

## When NOT to Use

**[Variant A]**

When technical constraints demand a strict deterministic outcome, or when organizational politics prohibit open expression.

**[Variant B]**

When fine-grained system behavior is needed, or when dominant personalities prevent equal group participation.

## Alternatives & Complementary Methods

**[Variant A]**

Alternatives include Mind Mapping, Collaborative Games, Focus Groups, and Focus Group/Surveys.

**[Variant B]**

Brainstorming Paradox, Method 6-3-5, Brainwriting, Mind Mapping

## Supported Decisions

**[Variant A]**

Solution option selection, risk identification, change strategy approach.

**[Variant B]**

Scope definition, product feature selection, vision alignment.

## AI & Agentic Adaptation

**[Variant A]**

AI agents can act as virtual participants in brainstorming sessions, generating 20+ divergent ideas instantly based on prompt criteria, which human facilitators then refine.

**[Variant B]**

AI agents act as idea generators or moderators that categorize ideas in real-time without introducing cognitive bias.
