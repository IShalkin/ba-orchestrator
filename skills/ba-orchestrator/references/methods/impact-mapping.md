# Impact Mapping

`T1 Strategy Analysis and Justification › 1.2 Strategy, Goals and Measures`

## Problem Solved

Prevents building the wrong software, waste of time and money due to wrong assumptions, scope creep, over-engineered solutions, lack of alignment with business objectives, pet features, ad-hoc prioritisation, and poor communication between business stakeholders and technical delivery teams.

## BA Lifecycle Phase

Discovery / Elicitation / Analysis / Prioritisation / Specification

## Input Artifacts

Business goals, shopping list of features/requirements, scope documents, feature lists, suggested user stories, or existing documentation.

## Output Artifacts

Impact Map (mind-map/diagram), metrics table, SMART goal definitions, milestone plans, and refined user stories.

## Participants & Roles

Senior decision-makers, business sponsors, product managers/owners, technical experts, architects, business analysts, delivery team, and workshop facilitator.

## Prerequisites

Access to key business decision-makers and senior technical experts; defined or discoverable strategic business goals; commitment to work towards measurable outcomes rather than fixed scope.

## Step-by-Step Procedure

Discover real goals by asking 'why' (using 5-Whys approach) until reaching business value (save/earn/protect money).
2. Define good measurements using scale, meter, benchmark, constraint, and target.
3. Plan the first milestone, focusing on a single driving objective using dot-voting or virtual cash.
4. Draw the map skeleton by connecting the milestone goal to key high-level deliverables, identifying underlying actors and impacts.
5. Find alternative actors and impacts through divergent thinking and brainstorming.
6. Identify key priorities by focusing on low-hanging fruit, critical obstructions, or crucial assumptions.
7. Earn or learn: define deliverables as options or small experiments fitted to a predefined learning budget.

## Analyst Questions & Prompts

Why are we doing this? How does this save, earn, or protect money? 2. Who can produce the desired effect? Who can obstruct it? Who are the users/consumers? 3. How should our actors' behaviour change? How can they help us or obstruct us? 4. What can we do to support the required impacts? 5. If we achieve the key targets for metrics with a completely different scope than planned, have we succeeded? 6. What else could those guys do for us? Who else can help? Who can obstruct us? 7. What is the simplest way to support this activity? Could we test it without software or start with a manual process?

## Minimal Realistic Example

In online gaming, to achieve the goal of 1M active players (Why), primary actors called Players (Who) help by Inviting Friends (How). The team delivers semi-automated invites or Facebook invite integration (What) to support that impact.

## Quality Criteria & Verification

Verify that goals are SMART (Specific, Measurable, Action-oriented, Realistic, Timely); ensure actors are specific (not generic 'users'); ensure impacts describe behavioral changes rather than features; confirm deliverables connect logically to impacts; validate assumptions iteratively through frequent releases using real-world metrics.

## Errors & Anti-Patterns

The Jumper (skipping impact levels), The Astronaut (mapping goals without metrics), The Surrealist (unrealistic metrics/incapable team), The Shopper (mapping out entire feature shopping lists up front), The Optimist (ignoring negative impacts/obstructions), The Dreamer (pursuing impacts unaligned with goals), The Robot (assuming all solutions must be software), and The Octopus (trying to map too many goals at once).

## When NOT to Use

Not applicable to maintenance-mode projects without strategic goals, projects with no access to business decision-makers, or environments with rigid fixed-scope/fixed-cost contracts that forbid adaptive planning.

## Alternatives & Complementary Methods

InUse Effect Mapping, HET method, Feature Injection, Planguage, SMART criteria, User Story Mapping, Hamburger Method, Kano Model, Purpose Alignment Model, Lean Startup (Build-Measure-Learn), and I* model.

## Supported Decisions

Scope decisions, milestone roadmap decisions, feature prioritization, architecture/solution design trade-offs, release planning, and investment allocation (incremental funding).
