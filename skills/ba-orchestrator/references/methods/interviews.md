# Interviews

`T2 Situation Investigation and Elicitation › 2.5 Interviews, Questionnaires and Individual Techniques`

**Also described as:** Interview; Interviewing; Structured Stakeholder Interviews

This card merges 8 descriptions of the method, labelled Variant A–H in every field.

## Problem Solved

**[Variant A]**

Reduces uncertainty, unstated expectations, and lack of clarity by gathering deep qualitative insights, real-life practices, personal complaints, and improvement ideas directly from key people.

**[Variant B]**

Reduces tacit knowledge gaps, uncovers personal complaints/expectations, and collects deep qualitative domain knowledge directly from stakeholders.

**[Variant C]**

Reduces requirement gaps and stakeholder misalignment by directly eliciting business analysis information, concerns, and goals through structured or unstructured dialogue.

**[Variant D]**

Elicits qualitative facts, opinions, needs, tacit knowledge, and political perspectives through structured one-to-one discussions, reducing stakeholder misunderstanding and resistance.

**[Variant E]**

Elicits explicit stakeholder needs, personal perspectives, and detailed job knowledge directly from individual domain experts.

**[Variant F]**

Elicits precise and unbiased statements; uncovers subconscious requirements through clever questioning.

**[Variant G]**

Reduces ambiguity and silence regarding individual role-specific domain knowledge, user workflows, and hidden operational constraints.

**[Variant H]**

Unclear requirements and hidden problems

## Prerequisites

**[Variant A]**

Target stakeholders identified; interviewer has acquired preliminary domain context via background study.

**[Variant B]**

Stakeholder identification, background study to avoid asking basic/ignorant questions.

**[Variant C]**

Clear interview goals, pre-identified interviewees, and scheduled logistics.

**[Variant D]**

Selection of interviewees, defined interview objectives, sent agenda.

**[Variant E]**

Identified stakeholder role, management permission, clear interview objectives, and background domain preparation.

**[Variant F]**

Predetermined questions prepared by the requirements engineer; interviewee willing to invest time.

**[Variant G]**

Identified stakeholder role, established objectives for the interview, prepared set of structured and open-ended questions.

## Step-by-Step Procedure

**[Variant A]**

Select the right stakeholder based on role, expertise, and exposure.
2. Prepare structured questions targeting specific goals/problems while planning open-ended prompts.
3. Conduct the interview: establish rapport, introduce context, ask easy questions first, explore 'why' behind decisions, avoid biased/opinionated questions.
4. Record and transcribe answers.
5. Synthesize input into structured reports.
6. Submit report to interviewee for review, validation, and refinement.

**[Variant B]**

Select the appropriate interviewee based on expertise and role.
2. Prepare a structured interview guide mix of closed and open-ended questions.
3. Establish rapport, state purpose, and get permission to record.
4. Conduct the interview, using structured questions first and following up on unexpected answers in unstructured mode.
5. Ask 'WHY' questions to uncover underlying goals and 'HOW' questions for operational details.
6. Document transcripts, synthesize findings, and submit the report to the interviewee for validation.

**[Variant C]**

Establish clear interview goals based on business needs.
2. Select appropriate interviewees and choose structured, unstructured, or hybrid format.
3. Design interview questions (open-ended for exploration, closed for confirmation) and interview guide.
4. Opening: explain purpose, roles, and how data will be recorded.
5. Conduct interview: ask questions, practice active listening, and record responses.
6. Closing: summarize main points, invite follow-up, and thank interviewee.
7. Follow-up: organize notes and send to interviewee for confirmation.

**[Variant D]**

Plan and prepare (agenda, venue, targeted questions).
2. Introduction and scene-setting (build rapport, state purpose, confirm note-taking).
3. Conduct main questioning (use open, probing, closed questions).
4. Summarize key points, gather sample documents, thank interviewee, explain next steps.
5. Write up notes promptly and send to interviewee for confirmation.

**[Variant E]**

Define interview objectives and select target stakeholders.
2. Prepare interview guide using open-ended prompts (Kipling's Who, What, Why, When, Where, How).
3. Conduct interview in pairs (one primary interviewer, one scribe).
4. Use neutral, non-leading questions; play back understanding continuously.
5. Sketch real-time informal models (flowcharts, goal bubbles) during the session to confirm concepts.
6. Transcribe/clean notes immediately after the session.
7. Send summary report to interviewee for review, correction, and sign-off.

**[Variant F]**

Prepare predetermined questions.
2. Conduct conversation with one or more stakeholders.
3. Ask questions and record answers.
4. Control conversation flow and adapt to individual stakeholders.
5. Ask follow-up/clever questions to uncover subconscious requirements.
6. Discuss and clarify immediate questions or doubts.

**[Variant G]**

Define interview objectives, specific role context, and target duration (1-2 hours).
2. Prepare structured question guide containing both specific closed questions and context-free open questions.
3. Schedule meeting and confirm attendance.
4. Conduct interview: stick to time box, maintain an egoless attitude, ask basic/probing questions, and explore the 'unsaid'.
5. Save time for meta-questions ('What else should I be asking you?').
6. Produce an egoless Interview Report focusing on key domain lessons rather than verbatim transcript.
7. Validate findings with interviewee and integrate into repository (P.7).

**[Variant H]**

1. Prepare questions.
2. Schedule the meeting.
3. Conduct the interview.
4. Record the results.
5. Agree the minutes.

## Analyst Questions & Prompts

**[Variant A]**

Why is this step performed? What problems do you encounter daily? How would you like this issue to be addressed? What if this exception occurs?

**[Variant B]**

Why do you perform this task in this way? What are the biggest bottlenecks in your current workflow? What would happen if information X is missing?

**[Variant C]**

"Why do you perform step X in your process?", "What challenges do you face with the current system?", "What would success look like for this solution?"

**[Variant D]**

Please tell me about your daily process? What are your key pain points? What happens if an exception occurs? How would you like the new system to work?

**[Variant E]**

What do you do in your role? Why is that step necessary? Talk me through how you handle this task. What happens when an error occurs?

**[Variant F]**

Predetermined questions regarding requirements and immediate follow-up inquiries on specific domain aspects.

**[Variant G]**

"What is the goal of this task? What happens when an error occurs? How do you know when you are done? Is there anything else I should be asking?"

**[Variant H]**

'Why is this needed?', 'What is the main problem that occurs?', 'Who makes the decisions?'

## Minimal Realistic Example

**[Variant A]**

An analyst interviews a chief librarian to discover why duplicate journal subscriptions occur and extracts the goal of centralized subscription management.

**[Variant B]**

Interviewing a head librarian to discover that manual duplicate checking during book acquisition wastes 15 hours a week, yielding the goal 'Automate duplicate acquisition checks.'

**[Variant C]**

A 45-minute structured interview with a Claims Manager uncovers 5 unwritten business rules governing high-value insurance claim approvals.

**[Variant D]**

A BA interviews an order processing supervisor at their desk to understand manual entry bottlenecks and collect completed sample order forms.

**[Variant E]**

An analyst interviews an accounts clerk about invoice processing, asking her to step through her daily routine and sketching a handwritten role/action list as she speaks.

**[Variant F]**

A requirements engineer interviews a traffic domain expert about route calculation parameters, asking follow-up questions to uncover unstated rules about road avoidance.

**[Variant G]**

Interviewing a Senior Payroll Specialist for 1 hour to uncover edge cases in monthly tax deductions for remote employees, discovering an unstated regional tax constraint.

**[Variant H]**

Interview with a warehouse manager to identify packing delays

## Quality Criteria & Verification

**[Variant A]**

High utility and coverage of acquired knowledge relative to time spent; post-interview transcript validation by interviewee; absence of interviewer bias.

**[Variant B]**

Interviewee review and sign-off on transcript summaries; cross-interview triangulation across multiple stakeholders.

**[Variant C]**

Interview results documented prompt after session; written notes confirmed/signed off by interviewee; objective non-leading questions used.

**[Variant D]**

Written interview notes validated and signed off by the interviewee for factual accuracy.

**[Variant E]**

Explicit sign-off and red-lining of notes by the interviewee, consistency with findings from other interviews, coverage of operational edge cases.

**[Variant F]**

Completeness of answers, clarity, and elimination of subconscious gaps verified through direct feedback during the conversation.

**[Variant G]**

Review of report by interviewee for correctness; extraction of verified requirements into Book E or S.

**[Variant H]**

Review with the interviewee, confirmation of facts

## Errors & Anti-Patterns

**[Variant A]**

Asking biased, opinionated, or affirmative questions; appearing unprepared or asking 'stupid' domain questions; failing to transcribe promptly; rambling without structured control.

**[Variant B]**

Asking opinionated, leading, or 'stupid' domain questions; failing to break the ice; interviewing wrong/unrepresentative stakeholders; failing to document transcripts immediately.

**[Variant C]**

Asking leading questions that bias responses; failing to validate interview notes with the interviewee afterwards.

**[Variant D]**

Asking overly leading or restrictive closed questions; failing to send an agenda beforehand; failing to write up notes immediately; taking no notes.

**[Variant E]**

Asking leading questions; interviewing subordinates in the presence of their boss; trying to record complex UML diagrams on a laptop during the conversation instead of actively listening.

**[Variant F]**

Overly time-consuming; surveyor-driven bias leading to forgotten or disregarded stakeholder concerns.

**[Variant G]**

Timidity (fearing to ask basic questions); selection and availability bias; conducting rigid, non-adaptable interviews; writing verbatim minutes rather than abstracting key requirements.

**[Variant H]**

Subjective answers, no clear goal

## When NOT to Use

**[Variant A]**

When target stakeholders are unavailable, or when quantitative statistical trends across thousands of users are needed (use questionnaires instead).

**[Variant B]**

When trying to discover complex physical procedural nuances that the user cannot articulate (use Observation instead).

**[Variant C]**

When eliciting consensus from large, disparate groups (>15 people) simultaneously (use Surveys or Workshops instead).

**[Variant D]**

When quantitative statistical data is required from a large, widely dispersed population (use Surveys instead) or for resolving multi-stakeholder conflicts (use Workshops).

**[Variant E]**

To discover multi-party business processes with complex cross-departmental conflicts (use workshops instead).

**[Variant F]**

When time and resources are severely limited, or when surveying an extremely large target audience where questionnaires are more appropriate.

**[Variant G]**

Resolving cross-departmental policy disputes (use Workshops instead).

**[Variant H]**

Low risk, known domain, urgent hypothesis with no access to people

## AI & Agentic Adaptation

**[Variant A]**

AI audio-transcribers can record and summarize interviews in real time, extract key requirements/goals using natural language processing, and flag inconsistencies against existing documents.

**[Variant B]**

AI agents can transcribe recorded audio interviews in real time, extract candidate goals/entities using NLP, and draft structured interview summaries.

**[Variant C]**

AI meeting assistants can record, transcribe, and extract action items and requirements from interview audio, flagging conflicting statements across multiple interviews.

**[Variant D]**

AI agents transcribe audio in real-time, generate automated summary notes, and extract entity-relationship or user story candidates automatically for analyst review.

**[Variant E]**

Agentic interview synthesis: Conversational AI agents conduct initial automated structured discovery interviews or transcribe human interview audio in real-time, extracting goals, entities, and requirements into draft models for BA validation.

**[Variant F]**

Agentic interviewers can dynamically ask follow-up questions based on real-time transcript analysis; human-in-the-loop validates complex context.

**[Variant G]**

AI agents can generate customized interview guides based on current gaps in the PEGS repository and analyze audio transcripts to auto-populate interview reports.

**[Variant H]**

Automatic transcription, summarisation, human-in-the-loop to check for hallucinations
