# Mind Mapping

`T2 Situation Investigation and Elicitation › 2.3 Boundaries and Context`

**Also described as:** Mind maps

This card merges 2 descriptions of the method, labelled Variant A–B in every field.

## Problem Solved

**[Variant A]**

Visually organizes and structures thoughts, concepts, and interview notes around a central theme, reducing information overload and unstructured note-taking.

**[Variant B]**

Reduces information overload and creative blocks by visually capturing and structuring complex ideas, concepts, and relationships in a non-linear diagram.

## BA Lifecycle Phase

**[Variant A]**

Discovery / Elicitation / Documenting Results

**[Variant B]**

Discovery / elicitation / analysis / planning

## Input Artifacts

**[Variant A]**

Interview agenda, workshop topics, brainstorming notes.

**[Variant B]**

Central thought/topic, brainstorming ideas, elicitation notes.

## Output Artifacts

**[Variant A]**

Mind Map diagram showing central concept, main branches, sub-branches, and cross-connections.

**[Variant B]**

Mind map diagram (main topic, branches, sub-topics, keywords, visual icons, color groupings).

## Participants & Roles

**[Variant A]**

BA (Note-taker/Facilitator), Workshop Participants / Interviewee.

**[Variant B]**

BA (author/facilitator), Stakeholders, SMEs, Team Members.

## Prerequisites

**[Variant A]**

A defined central concept or agenda topic.

**[Variant B]**

A central topic or complex problem statement to explore.

## Step-by-Step Procedure

**[Variant A]**

Place the central concept in the middle ('trunk').
2. Draw major related themes radiating outwards as main 'branches'.
3. Add detailed ideas as sub-branches ('twigs').
4. Use keywords, visual icons, and colors.
5. Draw cross-linking arrows between related sub-branches.

**[Variant B]**

Place the main central topic or image in the center of the canvas.
2. Draw primary branches radiating outward for key secondary topics.
3. Add sub-branches for detailed sub-topics radiating from secondary topics.
4. Assign a single keyword or image to each branch to explain the connection.
5. Use colors and visual symbols to categorize, prioritize, and group related branches.
6. Review and consolidate map structure to present complex concepts clearly.

## Analyst Questions & Prompts

**[Variant A]**

What are the main categories related to this topic? What sub-details belong to this branch? How does this concept connect to that branch?

**[Variant B]**

"What are the main categories radiating from our core business problem?", "How does sub-topic Y connect to main branch X?", "What keyword best describes this relationship?"

## Minimal Realistic Example

**[Variant A]**

A BA structures a Mind Map for 'Making the Business Case' with branches for Situation, Options, Cost/Benefit, Risks, and Presentation.

**[Variant B]**

A mind map centered on 'Portal Redesign' branches into 'User Roles', 'Security', 'Content', and 'Integrations', with 'Security' branching into 'OAuth' and 'MFA'.

## Quality Criteria & Verification

**[Variant A]**

Clear hierarchical branching from central topic; concise keyword usage; visual clarity.

**[Variant B]**

Map clearly presents complex associations; concise keyword usage; visually engaging layout that aids rapid recall.

## Errors & Anti-Patterns

**[Variant A]**

Over-crowding branches with long textual sentences instead of keywords; forcing a rigid hierarchy where cross-links are needed.

**[Variant B]**

Over-complicating branches with long sentences instead of keywords; using mind mapping during strict linear estimation exercises.

## When NOT to Use

**[Variant A]**

When formal sequential system workflow specifications or strict database schemas are required.

**[Variant B]**

When strict formal notation standards (like UML or BPMN) are required for contractual specification.

## Alternatives & Complementary Methods

**[Variant A]**

Rich Pictures, Concept Maps, Fishbone Diagrams, Brainstorming.

**[Variant B]**

Alternatives/Complements include Concept Modelling, Brainstorming, Affinity Diagrams, and Scope Modelling.

## Supported Decisions

**[Variant A]**

Interview structuring, issue identification, workshop agenda planning.

**[Variant B]**

Exploratory problem scope definition, workshop agenda structuring, feature categorization.

## AI & Agentic Adaptation

**[Variant A]**

AI text parsers automatically convert meeting transcripts into interactive digital Mind Maps with auto-clustered thematic branches.

**[Variant B]**

LLMs can convert unstructured interview text into structured JSON mind map trees, auto-categorizing topics and generating visual node charts.
