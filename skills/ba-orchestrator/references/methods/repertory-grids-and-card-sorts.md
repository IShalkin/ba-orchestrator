# Repertory Grids and Card Sorts

`T2 Situation Investigation and Elicitation › 2.5 Interviews, Questionnaires and Individual Techniques`

**Also described as:** Repertory grid

This card merges 3 descriptions of the method, labelled Variant A–C in every field.

## Problem Solved

**[Variant A]**

Reduces ambiguity and uncertainty surrounding domain concepts, implicit categorization criteria, and tacit domain knowledge held by experts.

**[Variant B]**

Reduces ambiguity around domain concepts by helping stakeholders surface implicit categorization criteria, attributes, and mental models.

**[Variant C]**

Elicits stakeholder constructs, attitudes, and unstated criteria without introducing analyst bias or leading questions, reducing researcher-induced distortion.

## BA Lifecycle Phase

**[Variant A]**

Elicitation / analysis

**[Variant B]**

Elicitation / analysis

**[Variant C]**

Discovery / Elicitation / Analysis

## Input Artifacts

**[Variant A]**

Pre-elicited domain concepts, cards representing domain concepts (textual or graphical).

**[Variant B]**

Elicited domain concepts, index cards (textual or graphical).

**[Variant C]**

Identified list of domain elements (e.g., product lists, document types, process steps).

## Output Artifacts

**[Variant A]**

Concept-attribute matrices (Repertory Grids), categorized concept trees/taxonomies, candidate domain properties and requirements.

**[Variant B]**

Concept-attribute matrix (repertory grid), categorized concept clusters, candidate domain properties and requirements.

**[Variant C]**

Rating grid matrix showing personal constructs rated on a 5- or 7-point scale.

## Participants & Roles

**[Variant A]**

BA, SME, Domain Experts.

**[Variant B]**

Business Analyst (facilitator), Domain SME / Stakeholder.

**[Variant C]**

BA (Researcher/Interviewer), Domain Expert / Stakeholder.

## Prerequisites

**[Variant A]**

A set of core domain concepts has already been identified.

**[Variant B]**

A initial set of domain concepts previously identified during domain understanding.

**[Variant C]**

Identification of a representative set of domain elements to compare.

## Step-by-Step Procedure

**[Variant A]**

Prepare concept list or set of concept cards.
2. Ask stakeholders to characterize concepts with attributes and value ranges (Repertory Grid) OR partition concept cards into subsets based on their own criteria (Card Sort).
3. Elicit the rationale/properties explaining the groupings.
4. Classify extracted properties as descriptive (domain properties) or prescriptive (requirements).
5. Repeat sorting with new criteria if necessary.

**[Variant B]**

Provide the stakeholder with cards representing domain concepts.
2. Ask the stakeholder to partition/group cards based on his/her own criteria.
3. Probe the stakeholder for the reason for grouping specific concepts together to reveal underlying attributes/properties.
4. Classify each revealed property as descriptive (domain property) or prescriptive (requirement).
5. (For Repertory Grids) Construct a matrix mapping concepts against attributes with value ranges.
6. Repeat iterations to discover additional implicit domain structures.

**[Variant C]**

Identify domain elements.
2. Present elements in triads to interviewee and ask how two are similar and different from the third.
3. Define the resulting similarity/contrast construct scales.
4. Rate all elements against each construct on a 5- or 7-point scale.
5. Analyze the completed grid matrix for key patterns and priorities.

## Analyst Questions & Prompts

**[Variant A]**

Why do these concepts belong together while those do not? What attribute characterizes this group? Is this property a natural law (descriptive) or a desired rule (prescriptive)?

**[Variant B]**

Why did you group these three items together? What feature distinguishes this concept from the others? Is this classification rule always true or a desirable policy?

**[Variant C]**

Pick two items that are similar and tell me why. Which third item differs, and in what way? On a scale of 1 to 7, how does each item rate against this characteristic?

## Minimal Realistic Example

**[Variant A]**

A librarian sorts cards representing 'Books', 'Journals', and 'Proceedings' into 'Borrowable Items' vs 'Reference Only', uncovering the rule that reference items cannot leave the building.

**[Variant B]**

A stakeholder groups 'Meeting' and 'Participant' cards together, surfacing the prescriptive requirement that 'the scheduler must know participant constraints prior to date selection.'

**[Variant C]**

A warehouse manager compares products using triads, uncovering unstated storage criteria: Weight (Light vs Heavy) and Shelf-life (1 Day vs Indefinite).

## Quality Criteria & Verification

**[Variant A]**

Consistency of classification criteria across iterations; expert validation of elicited domain properties.

**[Variant B]**

Verification of derived properties with other SMEs, consistency checks across derived taxonomy trees.

**[Variant C]**

Constructs must originate entirely from the interviewee's expressions without analyst prompting.

## Errors & Anti-Patterns

**[Variant A]**

Generating overly subjective taxonomies that do not reflect operational reality; complexity explosion when managing excessively large concept sets.

**[Variant B]**

Generating overly complex grids that are hard to manage; accepting subjective classifications as absolute domain facts without SME cross-verification.

**[Variant C]**

Imposing pre-defined construct scales on the interviewee; choosing elements that are too abstract or unfamiliar to the respondent.

## When NOT to Use

**[Variant A]**

When dealing with large, highly complex concept spaces without tool support, or when domain concepts are already standardized.

**[Variant B]**

When dealing with large, unmanageable sets of concepts without clear boundaries, or when concepts are strictly regulated and standardized.

**[Variant C]**

When rapid elicitation is required under strict time constraints, or when stakeholders are uncomfortable with abstract psychological frameworks.

## Alternatives & Complementary Methods

**[Variant A]**

Conceptual laddering, background study, class diagrams (object modeling).

**[Variant B]**

Complementary: Conceptual Laddering, Entity-Relationship Modeling. Alternative: Background Study of official taxonomies.

**[Variant C]**

Interviewing, Workshops, Surveys, Personas.

## Supported Decisions

**[Variant A]**

Domain concept structuring, taxonomy definition, initial rule classification.

**[Variant B]**

Domain conceptualization, data model structuring, entity/attribute definitions.

**[Variant C]**

User interface design criteria, process prioritization, document management classification.

## AI & Agentic Adaptation

**[Variant A]**

LLMs can propose candidate concept groupings and attributes from domain glossaries for expert refinement during sorting sessions.

**[Variant B]**

LLM agents can perform automated clustering of domain terms and suggest potential taxonomy trees for SME review.

**[Variant C]**

AI tools process completed grid matrices using cluster analysis to discover hidden semantic categories and user preference vectors.
