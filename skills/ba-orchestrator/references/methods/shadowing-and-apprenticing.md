# Shadowing and Apprenticing

`T2 Situation Investigation and Elicitation › 2.7 Observation and Immersion`

**Also described as:** Apprenticing; Apprenticing (Job Shadowing); Shadowing

This card merges 3 descriptions of the method, labelled Variant A–C in every field.

## Problem Solved

**[Variant A]**

Provides an extended form of observation where the analyst follows a worker through their entire role, reducing misunderstandings of role complexity and environmental pressures.

**[Variant B]**

Uncovers tacit knowledge and operational details that users cannot articulate in conference rooms or formal interviews.

**[Variant C]**

Overcomes power dynamics and uncovers deeply ingrained, self-evident domain knowledge by immersing the analyst in the user role.

## BA Lifecycle Phase

**[Variant A]**

Discovery / Elicitation

**[Variant B]**

Elicitation

**[Variant C]**

Elicitation

## Input Artifacts

**[Variant A]**

Role descriptions, observation checklist, project brief.

**[Variant B]**

Observer notes, current process sketches, task logs.

**[Variant C]**

Active operational work environment

## Output Artifacts

**[Variant A]**

Detailed role execution logs, qualitative issue list, identified environmental constraints.

**[Variant B]**

Detailed Task Models, Current-State Scenarios (How-Now), Exception Logs, Discovered Business Rules.

**[Variant C]**

Detailed domain knowledge notes, unstated operational requirements

## Participants & Roles

**[Variant A]**

BA (Shadow), Business Worker (Subject), Line Manager.

**[Variant B]**

Business Analyst (Apprentice), User/SME (Master Craftsman).

**[Variant C]**

Requirements Engineer (Apprentice), Domain Specialist (Master)

## Prerequisites

**[Variant A]**

Permission from managers, trade unions (if applicable), and subject; agreed study etiquette.

**[Variant B]**

Access to the operational workplace and permission to observe live work.

**[Variant C]**

Willingness of the domain expert to act as instructor; time for active hands-on training.

## Step-by-Step Procedure

**[Variant A]**

Identify candidate complex jobs.
2. Explain study objectives and build rapport with subject.
3. Shadow the subject continuously over days/weeks, taking notes.
4. Observe customer/peer interactions and environmental factors.
5. Ask clarifying questions at appropriate non-intrusive moments.
6. Analyze qualitative findings.

**[Variant B]**

Sit with the user at their actual workplace during normal operations.
2. Observe the user performing real daily tasks.
3. Listen to the user's running commentary on what they are doing and why.
4. Ask clarifying questions on the fly ("Why did you do that?", "What happens if this data is missing?").
5. Sketch process models or scenarios while observing.
6. Validate the models with the user and abstract essential needs from physical workarounds.

**[Variant C]**

Position the domain expert as the 'master' and the requirements engineer as the 'apprentice.'
2. The master instructs the analyst on how to perform the job tasks.
3. The analyst actively performs the work steps.
4. The analyst asks questions regarding complex or confusing tasks as they arise.
5. The analyst documents taken-for-granted rules and formulates requirements.

## Analyst Questions & Prompts

**[Variant A]**

What pressures are you experiencing right now? How do you decide how to handle this difficult customer? Why did you use that shortcut?

**[Variant B]**

"Why did you perform that specific action?", "What does this code/value mean?", "How often does this exception happen and how do you handle it?", "Where do you get the information needed for this step?"

**[Variant C]**

"Could you teach me how to handle this case?", "Why is this step necessary before moving to the next?"

## Minimal Realistic Example

**[Variant A]**

A BA shadows an airport lost-baggage clerk for three days to experience firsthand customer hostility, system lag, and multi-tasking pressures.

**[Variant B]**

A BA sits alongside a customer service agent handling insurance claims, observing how the agent manually cross-references three different legacy screens to verify policy details.

**[Variant C]**

A BA spends three days learning to dispatch service vehicles under the guidance of a senior dispatcher to understand implicit prioritization rules.

## Quality Criteria & Verification

**[Variant A]**

Shadowing sustained long enough for the subject to revert to natural, un-staged working behaviors.

**[Variant B]**

Discovered workflow reflects real-world operational reality (including informal workarounds) rather than idealized management procedures; models validated by the operator.

**[Variant C]**

Accurate grasp of domain rules; explicit documentation of unstated operational needs.

## Errors & Anti-Patterns

**[Variant A]**

Interfering with critical or sensitive customer interactions; making the worker uncomfortable; drawing statistical conclusions from a single subject.

**[Variant B]**

Interrupting high-risk/high-stress operations inappropriately; taking management's idealized process description as truth without observing actual workers.

**[Variant C]**

Becoming bogged down in operational routine instead of abstracting requirements.

## When NOT to Use

**[Variant A]**

When quantitative statistical time measurements are required (use Activity Sampling), or when studying highly confidential 1-on-1 counseling/banking tasks where observers are barred.

**[Variant B]**

High-security classified environments, hazardous locations, or emergency ultra-high-pressure tasks (e.g., active surgery, air traffic control during crisis).

**[Variant C]**

When domain experts lack time to train others or when safety/compliance prevents non-certified staff from performing tasks.

## Alternatives & Complementary Methods

**[Variant A]**

Observation, Ethnographic Studies, Protocol Analysis, Personas.

**[Variant B]**

Contextual Inquiry, Video Analysis, Interactive Workshops, Document Archaeology.

**[Variant C]**

Field Observation, Interviews

## Supported Decisions

**[Variant A]**

Job design, process redesign, user training requirements, workplace environment improvements.

**[Variant B]**

Usability requirements, current-state baseline verification, exception handling workflow design.

**[Variant C]**

User interface design, operational workflow scope.

## AI & Agentic Adaptation

**[Variant A]**

Wearable cameras or eye-tracking AI assistants summarize physical worker movement patterns and environmental interaction bottlenecks.

**[Variant B]**

Screen-recording and process-mining AI agents can record user desktop actions to draft initial task workflows; BAs use these AI-generated logs during shadowing to focus on asking 'why' rather than manually charting steps.

**[Variant C]**

Interactive simulation environments where AI agents evaluate user steps and highlight implicit logic gaps.
