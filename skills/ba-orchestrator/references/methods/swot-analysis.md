# SWOT Analysis

`T1 Strategy Analysis and Justification › 1.1 External Environment and Market Analysis`

**Also described as:** SWOT analysis

This card merges 3 descriptions of the method, labelled Variant A–C in every field.

## Problem Solved

**[Variant A]**

Consolidates and synthesizes internal strengths/weaknesses and external opportunities/threats, reducing unstructured, subjective strategy formulation.

**[Variant B]**

Reduces strategic blind spots by evaluating an organization's internal Strengths and Weaknesses alongside external Opportunities and Threats.

**[Variant C]**

No holistic view of the organisation's position

## Uncertainty Reduced

**[Variant C]**

Not understanding external threats and internal weaknesses

## BA Lifecycle Phase

**[Variant A]**

Analysis / Strategy Definition

**[Variant B]**

Discovery / Strategy / Solution Evaluation / change management

**[Variant C]**

Strategy analysis

## Input Artifacts

**[Variant A]**

PESTLE analysis, Porter's Five Forces, Resource Audit, MOST analysis results.

**[Variant B]**

Market research, current state assessments, industry trends, internal performance metrics.

**[Variant C]**

Market data, company strategy, interview results

## Output Artifacts

**[Variant A]**

SWOT Matrix, strategic options for development and evaluation.

**[Variant B]**

SWOT Matrix (2x2 grid: Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats), strategic action strategies (SO, ST, WO, WT strategies).

**[Variant C]**

Result: SWOT matrix (Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats). Artifacts: SWOT matrix, strategic initiatives

## Participants & Roles

**[Variant A]**

BA, Sponsor, Business Owner, Executive Management, Key SMEs.

**[Variant B]**

BA, Executive Sponsor, Business Owner, Strategic Planner, SME.

**[Variant C]**

Sponsor, BA, Business Owner, Management

## Prerequisites

**[Variant A]**

Completion of preliminary internal and external environmental investigations.

**[Variant B]**

Defined evaluation context (enterprise, division, project, or product).

## Step-by-Step Procedure

**[Variant A]**

Input identified internal positive capabilities as Strengths.
2. Input internal negative capability gaps as Weaknesses.
3. Input external favorable possibilities as Opportunities.
4. Input external risks as Threats.
5. Cross-analyze SWOT quadrants to derive actionable strategic options.

**[Variant B]**

Define the specific evaluation context and objective.
2. Identify external Opportunities (O) and Threats (T) in the market/environment.
3. Identify internal Strengths (S) and Weaknesses (W) of the organization.
4. Construct 2x2 SWOT Matrix.
5. Brainstorm matching strategies: SO (use strengths to exploit opportunities), ST (use strengths to ward off threats), WO (overcome weaknesses by using opportunities), WT (minimize weaknesses and avoid threats).
6. Summarize findings and incorporate into change strategy or business case.

**[Variant C]**

1. Identify strengths and weaknesses (internal factors).
2. Identify opportunities and threats (external factors).
3. Compare them and develop a strategy.

## Analyst Questions & Prompts

**[Variant A]**

What strengths enable us to seize these opportunities? What weaknesses make us vulnerable to threats? How can we overcome weaknesses to exploit opportunities?

**[Variant B]**

"What internal advantages or core capabilities do we possess?", "What external market trends present growth opportunities?", "What internal weaknesses expose us to external threats?"

**[Variant C]**

'What is our key advantage over competitors?', 'Which market trends pose a threat?'

## Minimal Realistic Example

**[Variant A]**

A retail firm combines its strong delivery fleet (Strength) with rising online shopping trends (Opportunity) to launch an express grocery delivery service.

**[Variant B]**

SWOT analysis for a retailer identifies Strength (Strong Brand) and Opportunity (E-commerce growth) -> SO Strategy: 'Launch mobile shopping app using brand equity'.

**[Variant C]**

Analysing an online retailer's entry into the grocery delivery market

## Quality Criteria & Verification

**[Variant A]**

SWOT entries must be explicitly derived from formal analytical models (e.g., PESTLE, Resource Audit) rather than ad-hoc opinions.

**[Variant B]**

SWOT entries are brief, specific, realistic, and supported by objective evidence; actionable strategies derived from combinations.

**[Variant C]**

Top management's expert assessment of how realistic the factors are

## Errors & Anti-Patterns

**[Variant A]**

Conducting unstructured brainstorming without rigorous inputs; treating tactical project responses as external opportunities or threats.

**[Variant B]**

Conducting SWOT at a vague level without a defined context; listing subjective opinions without factual evidence.

**[Variant C]**

Overly general wording, subjectivity not backed by facts

## When NOT to Use

**[Variant A]**

As a standalone technique without prior formal environmental and internal capability analysis.

**[Variant B]**

For micro-level software component specification where enterprise strategy and environment are irrelevant.

**[Variant C]**

Local technical tasks within an existing product

## Alternatives & Complementary Methods

**[Variant A]**

TOWS analysis, PESTLE, Porter's Five Forces, Resource Audit.

**[Variant B]**

Complemented by Benchmarking, PESTLE/5 Forces, Business Model Canvas, and Risk Analysis.

**[Variant C]**

PESTLE, Balanced Scorecard, Business Model Canvas

## Supported Decisions

**[Variant A]**

Strategic options identification, change initiative initiation, business case development.

**[Variant B]**

Strategic direction selection, change strategy formulation, business case justification.

**[Variant C]**

Scope, priority, business case

## AI & Agentic Adaptation

**[Variant A]**

LLMs can ingest multi-source analytical reports to draft a structured SWOT matrix, flagging ungrounded claims for human review.

**[Variant B]**

AI agents can analyze industry news and internal performance reports to populate a draft SWOT matrix and propose initial SO/WT strategies for executive review.

**[Variant C]**

Automatic collection of market analytics and news to identify threats and opportunities
