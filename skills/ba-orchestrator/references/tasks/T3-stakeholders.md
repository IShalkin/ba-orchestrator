# T3. Stakeholders

Selection guide: each line shows what the method solves and when NOT to use it (first variant; open the card for every variant's full text).

## 3.1 Stakeholder Identification

- [Stakeholder Identification and Analysis](../methods/stakeholder-identification-and-analysis.md) — Identifies all individuals or groups necessary for project success, along with their goals, risks, and potential resistance. *Not for:* None;
- [Stakeholder Nomination](../methods/stakeholder-nomination.md) — Identifies initial project stakeholders through direct recommendation from leaders and interviewees, reducing missed key project contacts. *Not for:* As the sole method for stakeholder identification without combining with structured tools like the Stakeholder Wheel.
- [Background Research for Stakeholders](../methods/background-research-for-stakeholders.md) — Uncovers hidden, external, or non-obvious stakeholders and organizational context by systematically analyzing existing project and organizational documentation. *Not for:* When no prior documentation exists for a completely novel greenfield startup venture.
- [Stakeholder Wheel](../methods/stakeholder-wheel.md) — Provides a comprehensive categorization checklist across 8 internal and external groups, preventing omission of critical external stakeholders. *Not for:* When analyzing a tiny internal micro-task with no external interactions or organizational dependencies.
- [Onion Model](../methods/onion-model.md) — Reduces omission of critical non-operational stakeholders (regulators, beneficiaries, negative stakeholders) and clarifies project context/boundaries. *Not for:* In extremely trivial or well-understood internal tasks where stakeholders are strictly fixed and known.
- [User Analysis (User Classes)](../methods/user-analysis-user-classes.md) — Identifies and classifies user populations into user classes, analyzing their characteristics, demographics, tasks, and working environments to guide UI/UX and work practice design. *Not for:* For purely automated back-end batch interface systems with zero human interaction.
- [Personas](../methods/personas.md) — Creates realistic fictional user archetypes to embody user goals, behaviors, and motivations, reducing selfish or biased user interface design assumptions. *Not for:* When designing fully automated back-end machine-to-machine interfaces with no human user interaction.

## 3.2 Stakeholder Analysis and Mapping

- [Stakeholder Analysis and Management (CATWOE, Power/Interest)](../methods/stakeholder-analysis-and-management-catwoe-power-interest.md) — Reduces political resistance, conflicting perspectives, poor buy-in, and communication breakdowns during change initiatives. *Not for:* When a change is strictly mechanical, non-contentious, and affects no human roles or responsibilities.
- [Power/Interest Grid](../methods/power-interest-grid.md) — Categorizes stakeholders based on their level of power and interest, reducing communication failures and resistance by establishing tailored management strategies. *Not for:* When working with a single business user on a localized personal productivity tool.
- [Social Network Analysis](../methods/social-network-analysis.md) — Maps informal relationships and communication pathways between individuals, reducing disruption caused by missing informal influencers and information brokers. *Not for:* In small 2-person projects or highly rigid automated environments with zero human interaction.
- [CATWOE](../methods/catwoe.md) — Uncovers and analyzes underlying stakeholder worldviews, values, and assumptions, reducing hidden conflicts, contradictory requirements, and strategic misalignment. *Not for:* For low-level, non-contentious technical coding tasks where stakeholder worldviews are identical and unproblematic.
- [RASCI Chart](../methods/rasci-chart.md) — Maps stakeholder roles and responsibilities across project tasks or business processes, reducing role confusion, unassigned ownership, and overlapping authority. *Not for:* For micro-level internal algorithmic coding steps with a single developer.
- [Empowered Decision Makers and Decision Rules](../methods/empowered-decision-makers-and-decision-rules.md) — Prevents decision paralysis, recurring rework, re-litigated requirements, unmanaged priority conflicts, and sluggish project governance. *Not for:* Always applicable;

## 3.3 Engagement, Conflict and Negotiation

- [Stakeholder Management Planning](../methods/stakeholder-management-planning.md) — Establishes structured, ongoing action and communication plans for each stakeholder, reducing project resistance and maintaining executive buy-in. *Not for:* In trivial, single-person projects with no wider organizational impact.
- [Thomas-Kilmann Conflict Mode Instrument](../methods/thomas-kilmann-conflict-mode-instrument.md) — Identifies five conflict resolution modes (Avoiding, Accommodating, Competing, Compromising, Collaborating) based on assertiveness and cooperativeness, reducing unhelpful conflict handling. *Not for:* When technical specifications are governed by strict non-negotiable legal regulations.
- [Principled Negotiation](../methods/principled-negotiation.md) — Resolves stakeholder disagreements by focusing on interests rather than positions, separating people from the problem, and using objective criteria, reducing destructive positional bargaining. *Not for:* When dealing with strict non-negotiable statutory legal mandates.
- [Conflict Management and Resolution](../methods/conflict-management-and-resolution.md) — Reduces contradiction, divergence, and misalignment among multi-stakeholder requirements and non-functional concerns. *Not for:* When statements do not overlap or when inconsistencies represent trivial terminology clashes (which should be solved via glossaries).
- [Six Thinking Hats](../methods/six-thinking-hats.md) — Reduces individual bias and overcome cognitive gridlock by forcing stakeholders to view a problem from six distinct standpoints. *Not for:* When detailed, fine-grained technical requirements need to be specified.
- [Consider-All-Facts (CAF)](../methods/consider-all-facts-caf.md) — Gathers all influencing factors surrounding a requirement conflict to prevent missing hidden drivers during negotiation. *Not for:* When time constraints require an immediate executive decision (overruling).
- [Outcome Frame](../methods/outcome-frame.md) — Shifts planning and negotiation mindsets from problem/blame orientation to a positive, future-focused outcome orientation (What do I want?, How will I know?, What resources?). *Not for:* When performing forensic root-cause auditing of past financial fraud where identifying past problem culpability is mandatory.
