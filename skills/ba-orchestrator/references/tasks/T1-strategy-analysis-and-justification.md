# T1. Strategy Analysis and Justification

Selection guide: each line shows what the method solves and when NOT to use it (first variant; open the card for every variant's full text).

## 1.1 External Environment and Market Analysis

- [PESTLE Analysis](../methods/pestle-analysis.md) — Provides a framework for investigating and analyzing external macro-environmental influences operating outside an organization's control, reducing uncertainty regarding future environmental threats and opportunities. *Not for:* When analysing short-term operational tweaks without external market or environmental dependency, or when immediate low-risk hypotheses need rapid execution.
- [Porter's Five Forces](../methods/porters-five-forces.md) — Determines the competitive intensity and profitability of an industry or market domain, reducing strategic blind spots regarding industry competitive pressures. *Not for:* In non-competitive internal process optimization or when analyzing internal team dynamics/operations.
- [Benchmarking and Market Analysis](../methods/benchmarking-and-market-analysis.md) — Reduces uncertainty about competitive standing, market trends, and organizational efficiency gaps by comparing internal performance against external baselines or market demands. *Not for:* When seeking novel, ground-breaking innovations (benchmarking only reproduces existing practices), or when fast action is required on immediate hypotheses without…
- [SWOT Analysis](../methods/swot-analysis.md) — Consolidates and synthesizes internal strengths/weaknesses and external opportunities/threats, reducing unstructured, subjective strategy formulation. *Not for:* As a standalone technique without prior formal environmental and internal capability analysis.
- [Boston Box](../methods/boston-box.md) — Evaluates product and service portfolios based on market share and market growth, reducing bad investment decisions in non-performing assets. *Not for:* For single-product startups or non-commercial internal IT systems without market competition.
- [Ansoff's Matrix](../methods/ansoffs-matrix.md) — Structures product and market growth strategies, reducing uncertainty when identifying alternatives for strategic expansion. *Not for:* When analyzing purely internal process optimizations or non-growth compliance projects.

## 1.2 Strategy, Goals and Measures

- [MOST Analysis](../methods/most-analysis.md) — Examines strategic direction alignment, ensuring tactics and operational efforts directly support higher-level mission and objectives, reducing strategic misalignment and conflicting priorities. *Not for:* When performing pure low-level technical bug fixing or minor isolated operational changes with no strategic impact.
- [Business Objectives, Vision and Scope](../methods/business-objectives-vision-and-scope.md) — Eliminates lack of alignment among stakeholders, prevents scope creep, and avoids building extraneous features that do not contribute to organizational value. *Not for:* When working on minor maintenance tasks, localized bug fixes, or minor refactoring where organizational strategy is unaffected.
- [Critical Success Factors and KPIs](../methods/critical-success-factors-and-kpis.md) — Defines vital areas of performance and specific quantitative metrics to monitor progress toward organizational objectives, reducing ambiguity in performance evaluation. *Not for:* When managing ad-hoc, exploratory research projects where quantifiable outcomes cannot be pre-defined.
- [Balanced Scorecard](../methods/balanced-scorecard.md) — Balances financial metrics with non-financial drivers of future success, reducing short-sighted over-reliance on past financial performance. *Not for:* For single minor feature enhancement projects without broad organizational reach.
- [KPI Modelling (KPIM)](../methods/kpi-modelling-kpim.md) — Reduces misalignment and wrong prioritization by directly quantifying process efficiency gains and linking requirements to measurable business metrics. *Not for:* When metrics data cannot be measured, or when process automation scope is trivial and metrics-driven trade-offs are unnecessary.
- [Business Model Canvas](../methods/business-model-canvas.md) — Reduces strategic misalignment by mapping on a single page how an enterprise creates, delivers, and captures value across operational, financial, and customer dimensions. *Not for:* For micro-level technical tasks or isolated software feature specs with no impact on the overall business model.
- [Value Proposition Analysis](../methods/value-proposition-analysis.md) — Analyzes customer perspectives on value across Timing, Financial, and Quality categories for Owners, Partners, and End Customers, reducing misalignment between business processes and customer expectations. *Not for:* For purely technical internal infrastructure maintenance projects with no customer-facing or commercial dimensions.
- [Opportunity Canvas](../methods/opportunity-canvas.md) — Reduces strategic uncertainty and risk of building features that fail to achieve business impact or solve real user problems; *Not for:* Small bug fixes, clear maintenance tasks, well-understood minor refinements with minimal cost and risk.
- [Impact Mapping](../methods/impact-mapping.md) — Prevents building the wrong software, waste of time and money due to wrong assumptions, scope creep, over-engineered solutions, lack of alignment with business objectives, pet features, ad-hoc prioritisation, and poor… *Not for:* Not applicable to maintenance-mode projects without strategic goals, projects with no access to business decision-makers, or environments with rigid…

## 1.3 Current State, Capabilities and Gaps

- [Resource Audit](../methods/resource-audit.md) — Identifies internal resource capability, highlights core competencies, and uncovers weaknesses that could prevent or undermine business change. *Not for:* In small-scale, rapid hypothesis testing where resource constraints are well understood or negligible.
- [McKinsey 7-S](../methods/mckinsey-7-s.md) — Ensures holistic organizational alignment across 7 core areas during business change, reducing implementation failures caused by misaligned soft/hard factors. *Not for:* For isolated, localized software tweaks that have zero impact on staff, structure, or management culture.
- [Leavitt's Diamond](../methods/leavitts-diamond.md) — Analyzes proposed organizational changes holistically across four interdependent components, preventing project failure caused by isolated technological or structural changes. *Not for:* For minor bug fixes or minor localized routine maintenance tasks.
- [Capability Modelling](../methods/capability-modelling.md) — Models what an organization needs to do to deliver value, independent of how, who, or where it is executed, reducing duplication and stability issues in business transformation. *Not for:* When analyzing short-term procedural task sequences or temporary project task steps.
- [Value Chain Analysis](../methods/value-chain-analysis.md) — Groups organizational activities into Primary (Inbound logistics, Operations, Outbound logistics, Marketing/Sales, Service) and Support activities, reducing siloed operational thinking. *Not for:* When analyzing micro-level algorithmic system code or localized single-task desk procedures.
- [Value Streams](../methods/value-streams.md) — Models end-to-end sequential stages that deliver value to stakeholders, reducing siloed process optimization that fails to deliver end-to-end value. *Not for:* For micro-level task step analysis or purely technical system integration mapping.
- [POPIT Model](../methods/popit-model.md) — Provides an holistic framework for identifying required business changes across four key dimensions, preventing single-dimensional (e.g. *Not for:* For trivial bug fixes or micro-level task edits that carry no broader organizational impact.
- [Organisation Modelling](../methods/organisation-modelling.md) — Integrates external environment factors (PESTLE, Competitors, Suppliers, Customers) with the internal Value Chain, providing complete organizational context for process improvement. *Not for:* When analyzing a minor, isolated internal software utility with no external environmental dependencies.
- [Gap Analysis](../methods/gap-analysis.md) — Compares the current 'as is' business situation with the conceptual 'to be' desired model to identify required change actions across Process, IT, People, and Organization. *Not for:* When the project is a pure technical infrastructure replacement with zero change to business processes, human roles, or organization.

## 1.4 Solution Options and Feasibility

- [Options Identification](../methods/options-identification.md) — Generates a comprehensive longlist of potential business and technical solution options, preventing premature commitment to a single unexamined solution. *Not for:* When the solution option is strictly dictated by mandatory statutory legal compliance with zero alternative choices.
- [Feasibility Analysis](../methods/feasibility-analysis.md) — Evaluates shortlisted options across Business, Technical, and Financial dimensions, reducing the risk of selecting unviable, unaffordable, or technically impossible options. *Not for:* During initial creative brainstorming where feasibility filtering would stifle idea generation.
- [Force-Field Analysis](../methods/force-field-analysis.md) — Identifies driving forces (+ positive) and restraining forces (- negative) acting upon a proposed change option, guiding strategies to strengthen drivers and reduce resistance. *Not for:* When selecting purely technical algorithms where human/organizational forces are absent.
- [Option Evaluation and Decision Analysis](../methods/option-evaluation-and-decision-analysis.md) — Reduces arbitrary or sub-optimal decision making when choosing among competing system proposals, goal refinements, responsibility assignments, or architecture styles. *Not for:* When there is only one feasible way to satisfy a requirement, or when options carry identical risk and quality profiles.
- [Plus-Minus-Interesting (PMI)](../methods/plus-minus-interesting-pmi.md) — Evaluates proposed solution alternatives objectively by categorizing positive, negative, and uncertain repercussions. *Not for:* When quantitative decision criteria (e.g., monetary weights) are strictly required.
- [Optioneering (Trade-Off Analysis)](../methods/optioneering-trade-off-analysis.md) — Selects the optimal system design architecture among competing alternatives that best satisfies conflicting stakeholder requirements within cost, risk, and technical limits. *Not for:* When only one technical solution is feasible or pre-mandated by law/policy.
- [Ready-Made / COTS Solution Selection](../methods/ready-made-cots-solution-selection.md) — Prevents organizations from buying ready-made software that fails to fit their business processes, or incurring unexpected, massive customization costs. *Not for:* Building 100% custom, highly proprietary core IP algorithms with zero commercial software equivalents.
- [Vendor Assessment](../methods/vendor-assessment.md) — Reduces third-party procurement risk by formally evaluating vendor stability, capability, licensing models, and compliance before committing to a commercial solution. *Not for:* When building an in-house custom solution with no third-party software, hardware, or consulting vendor involvement.

## 1.5 Business Case

- [Feasibility Assessment and Business Case Development](../methods/feasibility-assessment-and-business-case-development.md) — Reduces financial, technical, and business uncertainty before committing resources to a change initiative. *Not for:* When changes are minor, low-risk, or strictly mandated by legal/compliance requirements with no alternative implementation routes.
- [Business Case](../methods/business-case.md) — Reduces financial and strategic uncertainty by providing a formal justification for investing resources in a proposed solution compared to alternative options or doing nothing. *Not for:* For small, mandatory low-cost changes (e.g., urgent regulatory patch) where the cost of writing the business case exceeds the change cost.
- [Business Case Presentation](../methods/business-case-presentation.md) — Delivers a short, sharp, persuasive executive briefing to decision-makers, summarizing the business case recommendations and securing approval. *Not for:* When decision-makers require only a formal written report submission without a live meeting.
- [4As Communication Model](../methods/4as-communication-model.md) — Provides a 4-part planning structure (Aim, Audience, Arrangement, Appearance) for developing effective presentations and business documents. *Not for:* For casual, informal 1-on-1 chats where formal communication planning is unnecessary.
- [Cost-Benefit Analysis](../methods/cost-benefit-analysis.md) — Identifies, quantifies, and contrasts the expected costs and benefits of a proposed solution option across Tangible/Intangible and Immediate/Longer-term categories. *Not for:* When evaluating mandatory regulatory options where execution is legally compulsory regardless of cost-benefit ratios.
- [Investment Appraisal and Financial Analysis](../methods/investment-appraisal-and-financial-analysis.md) — Evaluates project options using quantitative financial models (Payback/Breakeven, Discounted Cash Flow / Net Present Value, Internal Rate of Return), proving financial viability. *Not for:* For non-financial mandatory compliance projects where options are dictated by law regardless of financial return.
- [Benefits Categorisation](../methods/benefits-categorisation.md) — Classifies business benefits into four distinct categories (Financial, Quantifiable, Measurable, Observable), providing a nuanced framework beyond simple tangible/intangible labels. *Not for:* When preparing a simple informal rough estimate with no formal benefits management framework.
- [Business Impact Analysis](../methods/business-impact-analysis.md) — Identifies and presents non-cost organizational, cultural, and procedural impacts of a proposed change, ensuring decision-makers have a complete picture beyond financial numbers. *Not for:* For minor isolated technical bug fixes with zero process or human footprint.
- [Risk Analysis and Management](../methods/risk-analysis-and-management.md) — Systematically identifies, assesses (impact & probability), and formulates responses/owners for risks threatening a proposed project option, increasing business case credibility. *Not for:* During initial unconstrained creative brainstorming sessions.
