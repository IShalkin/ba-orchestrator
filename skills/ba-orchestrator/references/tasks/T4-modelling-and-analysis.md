# T4. Modelling and Analysis

Selection guide: each line shows what the method solves and when NOT to use it (first variant; open the card for every variant's full text).

## 4.1 Business Processes and User Work

- [Business Process Improvement](../methods/business-process-improvement.md) — Reduces operational inefficiencies, bottlenecks, hand-off errors, waste, and poor customer experiences. *Not for:* When the business domain is entirely new and requires greenfield process engineering rather than incremental improvement.
- [Business Process Modelling (BPMN, Swimlanes)](../methods/business-process-modelling-bpmn-swimlanes.md) — Models cross-functional end-to-end workflows across actor swimlanes, reducing functional silo bottlenecks, handoff failures, and lack of customer focus. *Not for:* For static data modeling or high-level conceptual business activity mapping.
- [Process Hierarchy, Process Design and End-to-End Scenarios](../methods/process-hierarchy-process-design-and-end-to-end-scenarios.md) — Prevents fragmented, inconsistent process models and missed handoffs in large scopes by building a complete process taxonomy top-down, designing every process with one template, and validating the whole with end-to-end… *Not for:* The scope is a single process or one small change (model it directly with Business Process Modelling);
- [Activity Diagrams](../methods/activity-diagrams.md) — Models detailed task steps, decision paths, and iterations using standardized UML flowchart notation, reducing procedural ambiguity in task execution. *Not for:* For high-level conceptual business activity mapping (use BAM instead) or static data structure design.
- [Process Analysis](../methods/process-analysis.md) — Reduces operational waste, bottlenecks, and hand-off errors by systematically evaluating a business process for efficiency, effectiveness, and alignment. *Not for:* On highly ad-hoc, creative, non-repeatable human processes where fixed workflow analysis cannot apply.
- [Process Redesign Patterns](../methods/process-redesign-patterns.md) — Provides four structured analytical approaches (Reengineering, Simplification, Value-add analysis, Gaps/Disconnects) to guide process improvement and elimination of waste. *Not for:* When process rules are strictly mandated by external statutory laws that forbid procedural alterations.
- [Business Activity Modelling](../methods/business-activity-modelling.md) — Creates a conceptual model of activities required to fulfill a specific stakeholder worldview, independent of current operational execution, reducing legacy process bias during gap analysis. *Not for:* When analyzing low-level physical IT system interface interactions or detailed step-by-step procedure flowcharts.
- [Task Analysis and Task Descriptions](../methods/task-analysis-and-task-descriptions.md) — Defines the detailed execution steps, input/output data, triggers, prerequisites, and business rules for a single business process task, reducing operational ambiguity. *Not for:* For high-level corporate strategic option planning or macro value chain mapping.
- [Tasks and Support](../methods/tasks-and-support.md) — Balances domain-level user task demands against candidate software product features, bridging problem specification and solution evaluation. *Not for:* Pure technical sub-contracting projects where no human interface exists.
- [Business Event Analysis](../methods/business-event-analysis.md) — Identifies external, internal, and time-based triggers that initiate business processes, reducing missing process triggers and scope gaps. *Not for:* When modeling static data structures with no behavioral or event-driven requirements.
- [Business Use Case (BUC) Analysis](../methods/business-use-case-buc-analysis.md) — Reduces complexity and organizational bias by partitioning the problem space based on external events rather than internal department structures. *Not for:* Pure computational algorithms or batch processing modules devoid of external interactions.
- [Brown Cow Model](../methods/brown-cow-model.md) — Eliminates confusion during requirements discovery between current physical implementations and future essential requirements. *Not for:* Pure greenfield initiatives with zero existing legacy processes or completely novel product domains where no current baseline exists.

## 4.2 Data and Concepts

- [Entity-Relationship and Data Modelling](../methods/entity-relationship-and-data-modelling.md) — Reduces structural ambiguity, concept duplication, and fuzzy data definitions across domain concepts. *Not for:* For purely procedural/algorithmic problems involving no persistent conceptual data structures.
- [Class Modelling](../methods/class-modelling.md) — Models object-oriented data structures, attributes, associations, multiplicities, and operations, providing an enriched data specification for modern software architectures. *Not for:* When working in non-object-oriented legacy relational database environments where traditional ERD notation is preferred.
- [Data Dictionary](../methods/data-dictionary.md) — Reduces data inconsistency and misinterpretation across solutions and stakeholders by standardizing definitions, primitive elements, and composite structures. *Not for:* On small, single-use scripts or throw-away prototypes where formal data stewardship adds unnecessary overhead.
- [Data Expressions (BNF)](../methods/data-expressions-bnf.md) — Reduces ambiguity in nested, sequence-dependent, or composite data structures and message protocols. *Not for:* Describing large, non-sequential entity-relationship databases.
- [Data Flow Diagrams](../methods/data-flow-diagrams.md) — Reduces ambiguity regarding operational data transformations, inputs/outputs, and data dependencies between processes. *Not for:* When object-oriented encapsulated designs are strictly enforced and functional decomposition is forbidden, or for purely event-driven reactive state machines.
- [CRUD Matrix](../methods/crud-matrix.md) — Cross-references system use cases against data entities/classes to map Create, Read, Update, and Delete operations, ensuring data-process model completeness and consistency. *Not for:* In simple projects with under 3 use cases and 2 data tables where manual inspection is trivial.
- [Concept Modelling and Business Concept Mapping](../methods/concept-modelling-and-business-concept-mapping.md) — Reduces semantic ambiguity and vocabulary conflicts across stakeholders by organizing core domain terms and their structural connections independently of technical data design. *Not for:* For simple systems with universally understood, non-complex terminology, or where data design normalization is the immediate goal.
- [Decision-Driven Analytics Requirements](../methods/decision-driven-analytics-requirements.md) — Reduces uncertainty and feature creep in business intelligence and reporting projects by focusing on the specific decisions stakeholders need to make rather than just collecting lists of requested reports or fields. *Not for:* When the system requires standard operational transactional capabilities with simple, static reporting needs.
- [Report Specification](../methods/report-specification.md) — Reduces layout rework, missing query parameters, and unfulfilled business decision needs when delivering system reports and dashboards. *Not for:* When user needs are purely transactional and involve no data aggregation or reporting.

## 4.3 Behaviour and Interactions

- [State Machine Modelling](../methods/state-machine-modelling.md) — Reduces incompleteness and ambiguity in reactive component behaviors across all possible event occurrences and state transitions. *Not for:* For simple, non-reactive data-transformation applications without state-dependent behaviors.
- [Sequence Diagrams](../methods/sequence-diagrams.md) — Reduces confusion regarding the temporal sequence, message passing, and event ordering during component interactions. *Not for:* For specifying complete, exhaustive system-wide state transition rules (use State Machines instead).
- [Collaboration Diagrams](../methods/collaboration-diagrams.md) — Visualizes how software objects co-operate and exchange numbered message calls to complete a specific system function. *Not for:* User interface requirements elicitation or high-level business process modeling.
- [R-net Diagrams](../methods/r-net-diagrams.md) — Reduces ambiguity regarding all required parallel and conditional operational responses triggered by a single external stimulus. *Not for:* For non-real-time systems where stimulus-response dynamics are irrelevant.
- [Real-Time Behaviour Modelling and Event-Response Tables](../methods/real-time-behaviour-modelling-and-event-response-tables.md) — Reduces logical gaps, unhandled exception states, and timing hazards in hardware-controlling software by explicitly modeling allowed state changes, triggering events, and system responses. *Not for:* Simple CRUD business applications with no state-dependent logic or event-driven execution flows.
- [Behaviour Modelling via Scenarios and State Machines](../methods/behaviour-modelling-via-scenarios-and-state-machines.md) — Reduces dynamic ambiguity, timing mismatches, and unhandled event sequences by making explicit the temporal state transitions of agents. *Not for:* Simple batch processing or purely stateless query systems where temporal dynamics do not exist.

## 4.4 Goals, Obstacles and Rationale

- [Goal Modelling (KAOS / GORE)](../methods/goal-modelling-kaos-gore.md) — Provides a constructive, systematic method to build a complete, consistent, pertinent, and traceable multi-view system model combining WHY, WHAT, and WHO dimensions. *Not for:* For extremely small, low-risk, short-lived scripts or minor bug fixes where comprehensive multi-view modeling overhead is unjustified.
- [Goal Refinement Patterns](../methods/goal-refinement-patterns.md) — Reduces completeness uncertainty and missing requirements by reusing proven, standardized goal decomposition templates (e.g., satisfaction, safety, security). *Not for:* In novel, highly unstandardized domains where pre-existing category refinement patterns do not apply.
- [Obstacle and Risk Analysis (DDP, Fault Trees)](../methods/obstacle-and-risk-analysis-ddp-fault-trees.md) — Reduces requirements incompleteness, unhandled operational risks, and false confidence arising from over-idealized system assumptions. *Not for:* For non-critical features where obstacle occurrence carries minimal risk and cost-benefit analysis favors 'do nothing'.
- [Goal-Driven Object Modelling](../methods/goal-driven-object-modelling.md) — Reduces terminology ambiguity, missing conceptual state variables, and irrelevant domain clutter by deriving object models strictly from intentional goals. *Not for:* When building purely procedural or stateless scripts where structural state tracking is unnecessary.
- [Responsibility Modelling](../methods/responsibility-modelling.md) — Reduces unclear operational boundaries, unassigned goals, and system interface mismatches between software and environment agents. *Not for:* Single-agent monolithic systems where no interface boundaries exist.
- [Goal Operationalization](../methods/goal-operationalization.md) — Reduces the specification gap between declarative goals and executable functional services, ensuring operational specs correctly satisfy goals. *Not for:* When software functionality is entirely static or declarative without state transitions.
- [Rationale and Assumptions Modelling](../methods/rationale-and-assumptions-modelling.md) — Eliminates unjustified or 'gold-plated' requirements, prevents loss of decision history during staff turnover, and protects key requirements from arbitrary deletion. *Not for:* For standardized, self-explanatory, low-risk functional requirements.
- [Goal-Domain Tracing (QFD)](../methods/goal-domain-tracing-qfd.md) — Verifies that every business goal is supported by specific domain tasks/requirements, and that every specified task serves a business purpose. *Not for:* Small, simple projects where business goals and features are directly obvious.

## 4.5 Decomposition and Systems Analysis

- [Functional Decomposition](../methods/functional-decomposition.md) — Reduces system complexity and size uncertainty by breaking down complex processes, systems, or deliverables into smaller, independent, manageable sub-components. *Not for:* When analyzing highly complex adaptive systems where emergent behavior cannot be captured by simple hierarchical decomposition.
- [Interface Analysis](../methods/interface-analysis.md) — Reduces system integration failures and missing requirements by identifying where, what, why, when, how, and for whom data/messages are exchanged across solution boundaries. *Not for:* When analyzing standalone monolithic applications with zero external connections, human workflows, or hardware interactions.
- [Requirements Allocation and Architectural Partitioning](../methods/requirements-allocation-and-architectural-partitioning.md) — Reduces structural conflicts, integration failures, and cost overruns in complex embedded/real-time systems by systematically decomposing system-level requirements and allocating them across hardware, software, and… *Not for:* Pure software applications running on standard operating systems where physical hardware architecture is not part of the project scope.
- [Domain and Analysis Object Modelling](../methods/domain-and-analysis-object-modelling.md) — Reduces the risk of creating a purely functional, unmaintainable architecture by balancing dynamic behavior with static entity structure. *Not for:* In non-object-oriented procedural refactoring where static domain entity modeling adds no architectural value.
- [Object-Oriented Requirements Analysis with Contracts](../methods/object-oriented-requirements-analysis-with-contracts.md) — Prevents premature time-ordering overspecification, handles combinatorial case explosions, and provides stable, modifiable, and reusable requirements architectures. *Not for:* Simple procedural batch scripts with no complex domain object lifecycle.
- [Change Case Modelling](../methods/change-case-modelling.md) — Reduces architectural brittleness and costly rework caused by anticipated future business, legal, or technical changes. *Not for:* Disposable short-term prototypes or low-cost throwaway utility scripts.
- [Creating Requirements Models (Visual Analysis)](../methods/creating-requirements-models-visual-analysis.md) — Overcomes cognitive overload from dense text ('sea of details'), surfaces missing/conflicting logic, and bridges communication gaps with visual learners. *Not for:* When the logic, process, or data is extremely simple and can be unambiguously communicated in 1-2 plain text sentences.

## 4.6 Architecture Analysis

- [Quality Attribute Workshop (QAW)](../methods/quality-attribute-workshop-qaw.md) — Reduces uncertainty and ambiguity regarding architecturally significant quality attribute requirements prior to completing the architecture design. *Not for:* Small projects with trivial quality requirements or well-known, fully established domains without stakeholder uncertainty.
- [PALM (Business Goals for Architecture)](../methods/palm-business-goals-for-architecture.md) — Reduces uncertainty regarding business goals and aligns architectural decisions directly with business motivations. *Not for:* Projects where business goals are already explicit, fixed, and clearly mapped to technical requirements.
- [Quality Attribute Utility Tree](../methods/quality-attribute-utility-tree.md) — Solves prioritization conflicts and lack of specificity in quality attribute requirements by organizing and quantifying them. *Not for:* Extremely small systems with no significant or complex quality attribute requirements.
- [Attribute-Driven Design (ADD)](../methods/attribute-driven-design-add.md) — Eliminates design uncertainty and arbitrary decision-making by systematically designing software architecture based on prioritized quality attribute requirements and architectural drivers. *Not for:* Trivial systems where standard reference architectures can be directly instantiated without adaptation or trade-offs.
- [ATAM](../methods/atam.md) — Uncovers architectural risks, sensitivity points, and tradeoff points that threaten business goals before system deployment. *Not for:* Very early stages before any candidate architecture exists, or on small, low-risk systems where evaluation cost exceeds system risk exposure.
- [Lightweight Architecture Evaluation](../methods/lightweight-architecture-evaluation.md) — Provides a low-cost, fast-turnaround peer review mechanism to detect architectural risks during iterative development. *Not for:* High-risk, mission-critical systems requiring formal external validation, or initial architecture reviews of complex systems.
- [Tactics-Based Questionnaires](../methods/tactics-based-questionnaires.md) — Rapidly identifies missing architectural mechanisms and design risks for a specific quality attribute. *Not for:* When holistic trade-off analysis across multiple competing quality attributes is required simultaneously.
- [Architecture Debt Analysis](../methods/architecture-debt-analysis.md) — Identifies, quantifies, and calculates the ROI for refactoring architectural debt caused by design anti-patterns and high maintenance churn. *Not for:* New greenfield projects without sufficient commit or issue history.
