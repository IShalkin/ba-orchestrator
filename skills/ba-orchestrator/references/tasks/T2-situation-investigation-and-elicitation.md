# T2. Situation Investigation and Elicitation

Selection guide: each line shows what the method solves and when NOT to use it (first variant; open the card for every variant's full text).

## 2.1 Problem and Scope Framing

- [Project Blastoff](../methods/project-blastoff.md) — Prevents projects from rushing into presumed solutions, starting on vague foundations, or solving the wrong business problem. *Not for:* Trivial low-risk bug fixes, routine operational maintenance, or simple predefined feature tweaks with fully known domain constraints.
- [Situation Investigation and Problem Analysis](../methods/situation-investigation-and-problem-analysis.md) — Uncovers underlying root causes rather than symptoms of business problems, clarifies ambiguous problem statements, and resolves conflicting stakeholder perspectives. *Not for:* When the issue is purely technical with zero business impact, or the root cause is completely known and verified.
- [Understanding the Problem Before the Solution](../methods/understanding-the-problem-before-the-solution.md) — Prevents teams from building features or products that go unused or fail because they solve the wrong problem, address only symptoms, or jump prematurely to a suboptimal solution. *Not for:* When the problem domain and root causes are already fully understood, clear, and validated by data, or for trivial low-risk bug fixes.
- [Context-Free Problem Definition Questions](../methods/context-free-problem-definition-questions.md) — Reduces ambiguity, misunderstandings, and misdirections when identifying the real business problem rather than jumping prematurely to a technical or biased solution. *Not for:* When the core problem and scope are already completely verified, baselined, and legally/operationally mandated with zero ambiguity.
- [Problem Determination Game](../methods/problem-determination-game.md) — Resolves multi-stakeholder conflicts, reduces noisy symptom lists, eliminates out-of-scope/unowned requests, and isolates the single real business problem to solve. *Not for:* When there is already a single, clear, uncontested, and fully validated problem statement established.
- [Product Scope Formula](../methods/product-scope-formula.md) — Defines explicit boundaries between project and product, mitigating scope creep and establishing clear decision criteria for project approval. *Not for:* In extremely minor operational defect fixes where scope boundaries are completely self-evident and localized.
- [Scope Modelling](../methods/scope-modelling.md) — Reduces scope creep and boundary ambiguity by explicitly defining what elements fall inside and outside the limits of a change, solution, or business need. *Not for:* On open-ended exploratory research initiatives where rigid boundaries impede initial ideation.
- [Cynefin](../methods/cynefin.md) — Categorizes business situations into 4 contexts (Simple, Complicated, Complex, Chaotic) to guide the selection of appropriate leadership, analysis, and decision-making approaches. *Not for:* When the project approach and domain complexity are already thoroughly understood and fixed.

## 2.2 Root Cause Analysis

- [Root Cause Analysis](../methods/root-cause-analysis.md) — Prevents superficial symptom-fixing by systematically identifying and evaluating the underlying fundamental origin of a problem. *Not for:* For simple, known one-off glitches where root cause is self-evident without investigation.
- [Five Whys](../methods/five-whys.md) — Distinguishes superficial symptoms from underlying root causes during business problem investigation. *Not for:* When the respondent lacks direct operational knowledge of the broader process domain, or when the problem is purely technical/structural and requires automated…
- [Fishbone Diagram](../methods/fishbone-diagram.md) — Identifies and categorizes the root causes of a specific business problem or undesirable effect, reducing superficial problem solving that only targets symptoms. *Not for:* When the problem is simple, single-factor, and obvious without requiring multi-category diagnostic analysis.

## 2.3 Boundaries and Context

- [Context Diagram](../methods/context-diagram.md) — Defines the high-level scope and boundary of an IT or business system and its external interfacing actors, reducing scope creep and missed system interfaces. *Not for:* When detailed internal process workflow or internal algorithmic logic needs to be documented.
- [Problem Frames and Scope Diagrams](../methods/problem-frames-and-scope-diagrams.md) — Reduces uncertainty regarding system scope, software-environment boundaries, shared phenomena, and misaligned machine assumptions. *Not for:* When system boundaries and environmental interfaces are already rigidly fixed and fully specified.
- [Rich Pictures](../methods/rich-pictures.md) — Captures the holistic complexity, human relationships, emotions, culture, and structural issues of a business problem visually, reducing communication barriers created by long text. *Not for:* When communicating formal system specifications to technical developers or presenting to overly conservative formal stakeholders who reject non-formal diagrams.
- [Mind Mapping](../methods/mind-mapping.md) — Visually organizes and structures thoughts, concepts, and interview notes around a central theme, reducing information overload and unstructured note-taking. *Not for:* When formal sequential system workflow specifications or strict database schemas are required.
- [Domain Diagram Modelling](../methods/domain-diagram-modelling.md) — Provides a holistic, broad view of the current business process ('as-is' problem domain) to prevent point-solution failures, uncover hidden dependencies, and isolate root causes. *Not for:* When making an isolated, pure technical fix that has zero impact on business workflows or process worker roles.

## 2.4 Domain, Document and Data Research

- [Background Study](../methods/background-study.md) — Reduces domain uncertainty, lack of foundational business context, and improper use of domain terminology before interacting with busy stakeholders. *Not for:* Rarely skipped entirely, but restricted when documentation is completely absent, untrustworthy, or obsolete.
- [Document Analysis](../methods/document-analysis.md) — Systematically extracts data requirements, data structures, and business rules from existing forms, screens, and reports, reducing missing data requirements in new IT systems. *Not for:* When creating a radical greenfield system where legacy data models are obsolete and misleading.
- [Document and System Archaeology](../methods/document-and-system-archaeology.md) — Uncovers hidden, implicit, or forgotten business rules, data attributes, and requirements buried in existing documentation, legacy screens, and paper forms. *Not for:* Completely novel greenfield projects in brand-new domains with no prior artifacts.
- [Knowledge Reuse](../methods/knowledge-reuse.md) — Reduces elicitation time, incomplete requirements discovery, and reinventing the wheel by leveraging pre-existing domain structures, taxonomies, and patterns. *Not for:* For radical design projects or highly novel domains where no past experience, patterns, or abstractions exist.
- [Data Collection](../methods/data-collection.md) — Reduces uncertainty regarding system performance metrics, costs, usage trends, and non-functional baseline numbers that are not documented. *Not for:* When accurate operational metrics are already documented or when time constraints prevent deploying statistical sampling.
- [Activity Sampling](../methods/activity-sampling.md) — Measures transaction times and time allocation objectively across business tasks, reducing reliance on subjective staff estimates. *Not for:* When detailed qualitative reasons behind tasks are needed, or when sample populations are hostile to measurement.
- [Special-Purpose Records](../methods/special-purpose-records.md) — Gathers quantitative data on activity volumes and time spent directly from business users, reducing analyst observation overhead. *Not for:* When staff are uncooperative or when high-precision objective timing is required.
- [Data Mining](../methods/data-mining.md) — Reduces decision uncertainty by extracting hidden patterns, correlations, and predictive insights from large datasets. *Not for:* When available dataset volume or quality is inadequate, or when business decision logic is simple and fully known.

## 2.5 Interviews, Questionnaires and Individual Techniques

- [Interviews](../methods/interviews.md) — Reduces uncertainty, unstated expectations, and lack of clarity by gathering deep qualitative insights, real-life practices, personal complaints, and improvement ideas directly from key people. *Not for:* When target stakeholders are unavailable, or when quantitative statistical trends across thousands of users are needed (use questionnaires instead).
- [Questionnaires and Surveys](../methods/questionnaires-and-surveys.md) — Reduces uncertainty regarding stakeholder perceptions, subjective preferences, and priority distributions across a large or geographically distributed user base. *Not for:* When deep qualitative exploration, complex process discovery, or domain learning is required.
- [Repertory Grids and Card Sorts](../methods/repertory-grids-and-card-sorts.md) — Reduces ambiguity and uncertainty surrounding domain concepts, implicit categorization criteria, and tacit domain knowledge held by experts. *Not for:* When dealing with large, highly complex concept spaces without tool support, or when domain concepts are already standardized.
- [Protocol Analysis](../methods/protocol-analysis.md) — Uncovers cognitive decision-making processes and tacit knowledge by having users give a running verbal commentary while performing a task. *Not for:* For simple, highly repetitive physical tasks requiring no cognitive decision-making, or with non-articulate subjects.
- [Information-Gathering Plan](../methods/information-gathering-plan.md) — Prevents disorganized, haphazard elicitation, eliminates wasted stakeholder meetings, and ensures complete coverage of all required problem domain topics. *Not for:* Emergency bug fixes where the defect is already fully isolated and documented.
- [Equivalence Classification for Stakeholders](../methods/equivalence-classification-for-stakeholders.md) — Reduces the time and cost of interviewing large populations of process workers without risking loss of representative operational requirements or variance data. *Not for:* When the user population is small enough to interview every individual directly, or when role activities are completely standardized with zero variance.

## 2.6 Group and Creative Sessions

- [Requirements Workshops](../methods/requirements-workshops.md) — Brings key stakeholders together to discover requirements, build consensus, and make rapid decisions, reducing delay and conflicting requirements. *Not for:* When stakeholders are in deep toxic conflict requiring one-on-one negotiation, or when simple factual data collection from dispersed populations is needed.
- [Brainstorming](../methods/brainstorming.md) — Mitigates creative blocks and limited perspectives by producing a broad, non-judgmental set of ideas and options for problem solving or feature generation. *Not for:* When technical constraints demand a strict deterministic outcome, or when organizational politics prohibit open expression.
- [Brainstorming Paradox](../methods/brainstorming-paradox.md) — Identifies system risks early and establishes proactive countermeasures by focusing on unwanted events. *Not for:* When the team is trying to define routine user workflows or optimistic product vision features.
- [Creativity Workshops and Triggers](../methods/creativity-workshops-and-triggers.md) — Prevents teams from settling for pedestrian, incremental, or presumed solutions by systematically generating innovative, high-value candidate ideas. *Not for:* Strict regulatory compliance tasks where solutions are 100% legally prescribed with zero room for variation.
- [Focus Groups](../methods/focus-groups.md) — Reduces customer and user adoption risk by collecting qualitative feedback, attitudes, and emotional responses about a product or topic in an interactive group setting. *Not for:* When quantitative statistical proof is required, or when topic matter is highly sensitive/confidential so participants won't speak openly.
- [Collaborative Games](../methods/collaborative-games.md) — Reduces stakeholder communication barriers, hidden assumptions, and reserved participation by using structured game play to foster collaboration and creative insight. *Not for:* In rigid, highly formal corporate cultures hostile to game play, or when quick deterministic technical decisions are needed.
- [Hothousing](../methods/hothousing.md) — Accelerates requirement clarification and solution prototyping through an intensive 3-day off-site workshop with competing teams, significantly shortening delivery timescales. *Not for:* For routine, minor system maintenance or when key business users and developers cannot commit to 3 days off-site.
- [Design Studio](../methods/design-studio.md) — Reduces solution bias, single-designer bias, and unviable UI designs; *Not for:* Simple standard interface updates, well-established UI pattern implementations with no ambiguity.

## 2.7 Observation and Immersion

- [Observation](../methods/observation.md) — Gathers firsthand insights into actual business practices and uncovers tacit knowledge, reducing reliance on filtered or inaccurate verbal accounts. *Not for:* When studying highly cognitive, invisible mental work without verbalization, or when statistical quantitative task-time data is required.
- [Ethnographic Studies](../methods/ethnographic-studies.md) — Immerses the analyst in the working culture over an extended period to understand social context, implicit norms, and naturalistic working behaviors. *Not for:* When rapid requirements delivery is required on tight project deadlines with low budget.
- [Shadowing and Apprenticing](../methods/shadowing-and-apprenticing.md) — Provides an extended form of observation where the analyst follows a worker through their entire role, reducing misunderstandings of role complexity and environmental pressures. *Not for:* When quantitative statistical time measurements are required (use Activity Sampling), or when studying highly confidential 1-on-1 counseling/banking tasks where…

## 2.8 Prototypes and Scenarios for Elicitation

- [Prototyping for Elicitation](../methods/prototyping-for-elicitation.md) — Reduces uncertainty when stakeholders do not know or cannot articulate what they need until they see a concrete representation. *Not for:* Well-understood, highly standardized technical domains with zero UI/workflow uncertainty.
- [Feasibility Prototyping](../methods/feasibility-prototyping.md) — Reduces technical uncertainty, uncovers dependency risks ('dependency hell'), and verifies whether external technology components or unproven algorithms can actually deliver expected performance. *Not for:* Standard, well-understood technologies with proven track records in the organization.
- [Storyboards and Scenarios](../methods/storyboards-and-scenarios.md) — Reduces ambiguity, misunderstandings, and disconnects between abstract textual descriptions and real-world operational interactions across stakeholders. *Not for:* Should never be skipped entirely, but caution is needed to avoid over-relying on scenarios for capturing complete system-wide declarative rules.
- [Wireframes](../methods/wireframes.md) — Creates simple 2D structural illustrations of screen layouts focusing on content allocation and navigation without visual style distractions, reducing UI design confusion. *Not for:* For non-visual back-end systems or when testing complex interactive micro-animations (use high-fidelity prototypes instead).
- [Customer Journey Mapping](../methods/customer-journey-mapping.md) — Prevents poor user adoption and customer dissatisfaction by mapping emotional pain points and needs across end-to-end customer interactions. *Not for:* Internal backend system components with no direct or indirect human user touchpoints.
- [Usage-Centric Elicitation](../methods/usage-centric-elicitation.md) — Prevents feature-centric thinking that results in missing capabilities, gold-plating, unused functionality, and poor user experience. *Not for:* For pure middleware, background batch jobs, real-time embedded sensor routines, or hardware drivers where direct user interaction is minimal or absent.
