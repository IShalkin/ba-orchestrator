# WF-T3 — Stakeholders

**Use when:** you need to know who has a stake, who decides, who resists, and how to engage them. Run it early in any initiative, or whenever conflicts block progress.
**Not when:** the stakeholder set is fixed, tiny and fully known, and there is no conflict (see the cards' When NOT to Use).
**Brief must contain:** the initiative and its scope, any known sponsor or decision-maker roles, the org boundaries, and the sources (org charts, RACI tables, meeting notes).
**Deliverables:** a stakeholder register with classes and personas, a power/interest map, decision rights, worldviews and conflicts, and an engagement plan.

## Research questions (seed)
- Which roles appear in org charts, RACI tables, meeting attendee lists or ticket assignees? Record roles, and names only where the sources state them.
- Who approved or blocked earlier decisions, and what did they say?
- Which regulators, external partners or affected groups are mentioned?
- Where do sources record disagreement, escalation or "parking lot" items?

## Gates and checks
- The register lists **roles**. Personal names appear only when they are quoted from sources, with an `[E:]` label.
- Every high-power stakeholder has an engagement action.
- Conflicts are recorded with both positions and their evidence. The register does not take sides.
- Expectations stay in the expectations log. None of them is turned into a requirement here; that happens only through change control (WF-T6).

## Hand-offs
The register → WF-T2 (who to interview), WF-T6 (who prioritises and approves changes), WF-T8 (change impact by group). The expectations log → every later gate (SKILL.md, "Expectations at gates").
